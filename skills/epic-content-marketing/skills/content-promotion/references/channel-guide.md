# Platform and channel selection guide

Use this reference when choosing which rented channels belong in a content promotion plan. For each platform: who's there, what content format works, what doesn't work, and what the CTA should be.

---

## LinkedIn

**Who's there:** B2B professionals across virtually all industries. Decision-makers, individual contributors, executives. Highest concentration of any professional social platform.

**What works:**
- Text-only posts: high organic reach, no link penalty (LinkedIn suppresses posts with external links in feed; put the link in the first comment)
- Short narratives (200–400 words) about a specific professional insight or experience
- Carousels (PDF slides): good engagement for frameworks, step-by-step processes, or data visualizations
- Native video: better reach than external video links; short (90 seconds or under) outperforms long
- Polls: high engagement, useful for audience research

**What doesn't work:**
- External link posts without a strong hook (will be suppressed)
- Long-form "articles" (LinkedIn article format has minimal organic reach)
- Hashtag stuffing
- Generic motivational content (high competition, low differentiation)

**Best CTA:** "The full breakdown is in my newsletter" + link in first comment, or "I cover this weekly — subscribe link in bio."

**Posting frequency:** 3–5x per week for serious growth; 1–2x per week for maintenance alongside other channels.

**Content types by persona:**
- Solo creator or founder: personal narrative + specific insight
- Brand account: company case study + employee perspective + industry data
- B2B SaaS: product-adjacent insights, customer success angles, category education

## Twitter / X

**Who's there:** Media professionals, tech workers, crypto/web3 community, researchers, writers, policy people. General consumer population has declined since 2022.

**What works:**
- Threads: 5–20 tweets explaining a concept, process, or perspective. Most effective for education and building followers.
- Hot takes and opinions with a specific argument (not vague "here are my thoughts")
- Real-time commentary on industry news (requires timing and speed)
- Quote-tweeting with added perspective on relevant posts

**What doesn't work:**
- Pure link promotion ("new blog post, check it out")
- Multiple hashtags (feels spammy, doesn't help reach on X)
- Posting frequency below 3x/day if growth is the goal — the algorithm rewards volume more than most platforms

**Best CTA:** First thread tweet hooks the reader; last tweet links to newsletter or full piece.

**Posting frequency:** High-volume if growth-focused (5–10x/day is standard for serious accounts). Otherwise, threads 3–5x per week is sustainable.

**When to skip:** If the persona is not in media, tech, or niche communities that still use X, this platform is not worth the effort.

## Instagram

**Who's there:** Consumer audiences, lifestyle brands, visual content creators, B2C product companies. Less relevant for B2B content programs.

**What works:**
- Reels: highest organic reach by far. 15–60 second videos. Educational or entertainment format.
- Carousels: swipeable multi-image posts. Good for frameworks, tips lists, or before/after content.
- Stories: ephemeral content for behind-the-scenes, polls, Q&A. Good engagement for existing followers.

**What doesn't work:**
- Static image posts (reach has declined significantly)
- External links (not clickable in feed — only in bio or Stories for verified/eligible accounts)
- Text-heavy content
- Pure promotional content ("here's my new newsletter")

**Best CTA:** "Link in bio" for newsletter opt-in; DM automation for lead capture.

**When to use:** If the persona is a consumer and the content translates to visual or short-video format. Not recommended as a primary channel for B2B or text-focused content programs.

## TikTok

**Who's there:** Young demographics (18–34 primary), but growing across age groups. Consumer brands, creators, entertainment. B2B presence is niche but growing.

**What works:**
- Native short video (15 seconds to 3 minutes)
- Education + entertainment ("edutainment"): teach one thing in an engaging format
- Trends and audio: using trending sounds and formats for discovery
- Behind-the-scenes and personal authenticity

**What doesn't work:**
- Produced, corporate-looking video
- Long-form (over 3 minutes)
- Text-based content repurposed from LinkedIn or newsletters
- External link promotion (not clickable in standard posts)

**When to use:** If the persona is under 35, the content translates to video, and the brand has video production capacity. Not recommended for B2B or text-first content programs unless there's a specific reason the audience is on TikTok.

## YouTube

**Who's there:** Almost everyone — YouTube is the second-largest search engine. B2B and B2C audiences alike.

**What works:**
- Searchable educational content: "how to [do specific thing]" performs well in almost every niche
- Long-form explainers (10–20 minutes) for audiences that want depth
- Series format: a connected set of videos that encourage subscription and binge-watching

**What doesn't work:**
- Low production quality (lighting, audio problems cause immediate drop-off)
- No clear value in the first 30 seconds (the algorithm measures audience retention — start with the value, not the intro)
- Posting inconsistently (YouTube rewards channels that publish on schedule)

**Best CTA:** "Subscribe to my newsletter for the written version" with link in description. YouTube-to-email conversion is lower than other channels but audience quality is high.

**When to use:** When video is the right format for the content and the persona watches YouTube for education or information in this topic area. YouTube requires significant production investment — don't add it as a secondary channel without the capacity to do it well.

## Reddit / niche forums

**Who's there:** Highly niche communities organized by topic. Extremely high engagement within communities; zero tolerance for promotion.

**Rules:**
- Never post promotional content cold. Read the community rules for each subreddit before posting anything.
- The only acceptable promotion is sharing a specific piece of content that directly answers a specific question that already exists in the thread. Not "I wrote an article about this, check it out" — "I researched this exact question last month, here's what I found" with a link as supporting material.
- Build a history of genuine contributions before sharing your own content.

**What works:**
- Answering questions genuinely, with depth, and occasionally referencing your own work as a source
- AMAs (Ask Me Anything) in relevant communities if you have genuine expertise

**When to use:** When the persona has a specific subreddit or forum community where they already gather. The investment is relationship-building, not posting. This is a long-term play.

## Newsletter cross-promotions

**What it is:** An arrangement (paid or unpaid) where two newsletter publishers recommend each other's newsletters to their lists. Platforms like Sparkloop, ConvertKit Recommendations, and beehiiv Boosts make this automatic.

**What works:**
- Swap with newsletters that serve the exact same persona but cover different topics (no direct overlap in content)
- Paid referrals via Sparkloop: you pay per new subscriber referred; the referring newsletter earns per referral. Typical cost: $1–$5 per subscriber for quality lists.

**What doesn't work:**
- Swapping with newsletters that have different audiences (the subscribers won't convert)
- Swapping with newsletters that have low engagement (their recommendations carry no weight)

**When to use:** This is one of the most efficient subscriber acquisition tactics for email newsletters. Start unpaid swaps once the list exceeds ~1,000 subscribers; consider paid referrals once the list exceeds ~3,000 and subscriber LTV is understood.

## Podcast guest appearances

**Who it reaches:** The specific audience of the podcast you appear on. Highly targeted if you pick shows the persona listens to.

**What works:**
- Pitching shows where the persona is the primary listener (research the show's audience, not just the topic)
- Delivering genuine value in the interview — not a sales pitch
- Having a specific, memorable CTA: one URL for newsletter opt-in, or a specific free resource tied to what you discussed in the episode

**What doesn't work:**
- Guest appearing on shows with no audience overlap with your persona
- Pitching your product instead of sharing genuine expertise
- Weak CTAs ("you can find me at...") instead of specific, valuable offers

**When to use:** If the persona listens to podcasts, and there are 5–10 shows in the niche with audiences of 1,000+ listeners. Guest appearances are time-intensive but highly targeted — one good appearance can drive 50–200 new subscribers.
