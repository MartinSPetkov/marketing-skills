# Content promotion and distribution

The first principle of Pulizzi's distribution framework: own before you rent.

Owned media is content and audiences you control directly — your email list, your website, your podcast RSS feed. Rented media is any platform that sits between you and your audience: LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, Twitter/X, Substack (even Substack — you don't own the platform). Rented media can change its algorithm, reduce your reach, or disappear entirely. It has happened repeatedly. Your email list cannot be taken away.

This shapes the entire distribution strategy: rented platforms are funnels to owned channels, not destinations.

---

## Before building the promotion plan

Ask:

1. What is the primary content asset? (newsletter, blog, podcast, YouTube channel, etc.)
2. What is the current primary distribution channel, and how is it performing?
3. What platforms does the audience already use? (from the audience persona — if they don't have one, stop and build it)
4. What does the team have capacity to actually maintain? Promotion that doesn't happen consistently is worse than no promotion plan.

---

## The owned vs. rented hierarchy

### Owned channels (build these first)

**Email list:** The most valuable owned channel for most content programs. Direct access to subscribers, no algorithm, no platform risk. Every promotion strategy should point back to email list growth as its primary goal.

**Website/blog:** Owned. Content lives here permanently. SEO value compounds over time. Not subject to platform changes.

**Podcast RSS feed:** Owned. The RSS feed and the audience relationship are yours. The directories (Spotify, Apple) are rented distribution — useful, but secondary.

### Rented channels (use as funnels, not destinations)

**LinkedIn:** High organic reach for B2B content compared to other platforms. Best used to share excerpts or key ideas from owned content with a clear path back to the owned channel (newsletter opt-in, blog post, etc.). B2B audiences are here. The algorithm rewards content that keeps people on LinkedIn — work around this by making the excerpt valuable enough that following the link feels like the natural next step.

**Twitter/X:** Reach has declined significantly since 2022. Still relevant for some niches (tech, crypto, media, policy). Not a reliable primary distribution channel for most content programs.

**Instagram / TikTok:** Primarily visual. Harder to drive traffic to text-based content. Effective for brand awareness and for certain content types (video tutorials, behind-the-scenes, quick tips). Not the right channel for most B2B content programs.

**YouTube:** A search engine, not just a social platform. Video content with good SEO can compound over years. If the audience persona watches YouTube, it belongs in the plan — but treat it as a primary platform that requires significant production investment, not an afterthought.

**Reddit / niche forums / Slack communities / Discord servers:** High-trust, high-engagement, zero tolerance for promotion. The rule: add genuine value to the conversation first. Share a specific piece of content only when it directly answers a specific question that already exists in the thread. Never cold-post promotional links.

**Newsletter directories (Substack, beehiiv):** Useful for discovery if launching a newsletter on those platforms. Separate from email delivery — you can use a platform's discovery features while maintaining your own email list.

**Newsletter cross-promotions (Sparkloop, ConvertKit Recommendations, beehiiv Boosts):** Paid or unpaid arrangements to recommend each other's newsletters. This is one of the highest-ROI subscriber acquisition tactics for newsletters, but only works if the other newsletter's audience closely matches yours.

## Phase 1: Content repurposing strategy

One piece of content should not live in one place. Before building the promotion plan, map how each primary content asset can be adapted for other channels.

**Repurposing hierarchy:**

Produce the primary asset at full length and quality. Then strip it into platform-native formats:

- Long newsletter issue → LinkedIn post (key insight, 200–400 words) + Twitter thread (if relevant) + short-form audio clip (if podcast exists)
- Podcast episode → blog post (transcript cleaned up, not published raw) + LinkedIn post featuring the key quote + YouTube clip (if video recorded)
- Long blog post → newsletter excerpt with link to full post + LinkedIn carousel (key points as slides) + Twitter thread

The rule: every repurposed version should add value on its own platform. A LinkedIn post that says "Check out my new newsletter" with a link is not a repurposed version — it's an ad. Write a LinkedIn post worth reading that also happens to connect to the newsletter.

---

## Phase 2: Platform selection

Don't build a presence on every platform. Pick two rented channels based on where the persona spends time and where the content format fits naturally. Build those well before adding others.

For each selected platform:
- Define the format (what type of post works on this platform for this audience)
- Define the frequency (what can actually be maintained, not what the platform recommends)
- Define the CTA (what action does every post invite? Most should point back to the owned channel)
- Define the metric (what does success look like on this platform? Not followers — engagement, click-through to the owned channel)

## Phase 3: Promotion workflow

Promotion is not a separate activity — it should be part of the production workflow. Build a standard checklist that runs automatically after every piece of content is published.

**Sample post-publish promotion checklist:**

Day 0 (publish day):
- [ ] Email newsletter sent (if newsletter is primary channel) or newsletter excerpt sent with link to primary content
- [ ] LinkedIn post published (repurposed insight from piece)
- [ ] Twitter/X thread published (if relevant)
- [ ] Content scheduled for internal Slack / team channels

Day 3 (follow-up):
- [ ] Share in any relevant communities (only if there's a specific conversation it fits into — not cold promotion)
- [ ] Reply to any comments or reactions on Day 0 posts

Day 30 (evergreen review):
- [ ] Check if piece is ranking for any search terms (Google Search Console)
- [ ] If piece has evergreen value, schedule a second promotion push with a new angle (e.g., a follow-up post saying "the most common question I got on this article was X — here's my answer")

---

## Phase 4: SEO as a long-term promotion channel

Organic search is the only rented channel that doesn't require constant feeding. A well-optimized blog post can drive traffic for years without additional promotion spend. This doesn't apply to social platforms.

For any content program with a blog, the promotion plan should include:
- A target keyword for each blog post (not keyword-stuffed — one clear primary term)
- Internal linking: new posts should link to older related posts and vice versa
- Regular review of Google Search Console data to see what queries are already bringing people in (often different from the intended keyword)

SEO results take 3–6 months to appear for new domains, 1–3 months for established domains. Don't use SEO as the only promotion strategy — it's too slow at the start of a program.

## What doesn't work (tell the user directly)

**Posting to all platforms simultaneously:** Content that performs on LinkedIn does not perform as-is on Instagram, Twitter, and Reddit. Platform-native formatting matters. Posting the same text everywhere looks lazy and performs poorly everywhere.

**Social media as a primary audience-building strategy:** Algorithm reach on social platforms has declined every year for a decade. Building a 100,000-follower Instagram account and no email list means you own nothing. A 2,000-person email list is worth more than 20,000 social followers.

**Post-and-pray:** Posting a link to content once and calling it promoted. Promotion is a system, not a moment.

**Buying followers or list subscribers:** Purchased audiences don't engage, don't buy, and don't share. Engagement rate drops, platform algorithm penalizes, and the whole metric becomes meaningless.
