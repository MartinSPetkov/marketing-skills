# Example: Content Marketing Strategy

**Brand:** Northside Ceramics Studio (small business, pottery classes and retail)

---

## Business goal

Convert 80 new email subscribers per month who become paying class participants within 90 days, at a rate of 15% (matching current subscriber-to-buyer conversion).

---

## Audience persona

**Maya, 34, freelance graphic designer.** She works from home, lives in a mid-size city, and uses creative hobbies to decompress from screen time. She's tried yoga and painting but wants something more tactile and social. She follows makers on Instagram but has never taken a hands-on craft class as an adult. She worries she's "not creative enough" and that she'll be the only beginner in a room of experts. She would subscribe to content that makes the learning curve feel approachable — real stories from beginners, not finished-product galleries from experts.

---

## Content tilt

We are the only weekly email and video series for adult beginners in the American Midwest that shows the full, messy, imperfect reality of learning pottery from scratch — centering failures and small wins equally, not just polished final pieces.

---

## Content marketing mission statement

Northside Ceramics delivers a weekly email and short video series for adult creative beginners in [City] to help them feel capable and excited about learning a physical craft for the first time.

---

## Engagement cycle

| Stage | Content | Action |
|-------|---------|--------|
| Awareness | Short-form video (Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts): "Week 1 as a complete beginner" series | Watch → follow account |
| Consideration | Behind-the-scenes blog posts: "What really happens in your first class" | Read → land on site |
| Subscription | Free guide: "5 things I wish I knew before my first pottery class" | Download → join email list |
| Conversion | Email sequence: Class schedule + student success stories + intro offer | Sign up for first class |
| Loyalty/Advocacy | Monthly email: "Student spotlight" — one student's progress over 3 months | Share with friends, refer others |

---

## Channel plan

| Platform | Content type | Frequency | Purpose |
|----------|-------------|-----------|---------|
| Email newsletter | "Beginner dispatch" — 300-word updates + one video | Weekly | Own the relationship; primary conversion engine |
| Website/blog | Longer posts, SEO-targeted ("is pottery hard to learn?") | 2x/month | Discovery via search |
| Instagram | Reels (messy process videos, not finished pieces) | 3x/week | New audience discovery |
| YouTube Shorts | Cross-posted from Instagram | 3x/week | Secondary discovery |

---

## Six-principles check

- **Fill a need:** Beginners are underserved. Most ceramics content shows expert work. Maya can't find honest beginner content — we fill that gap.
- **Consistent:** Weekly email is sustainable with one staff member and a 2-hour weekly content block.
- **Human:** Voice is warm, self-deprecating, and honest about mistakes. Written in first person from the studio owner.
- **Point of view:** We argue that imperfect learning is the point — "finished pieces are a byproduct, not the goal." Takes a clear position.
- **No sales speak:** Editorial content never promotes specific classes. The email footer links to the schedule, but body content is pure value.
- **Best of breed:** Within our city + beginner niche, we can be the definitive source. The niche is tight enough to win.

---

## What success looks like

80 net new email subscribers per month by month 6. At 15% conversion, that's 12 new class participants monthly from the content program alone — offsetting paid advertising spend by approximately 40%.
