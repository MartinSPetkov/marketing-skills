# The Six Principles of Epic Content Marketing

From *Epic Content Marketing* by Joe Pulizzi and Brian Piper (2nd ed., 2023), Chapter 6.

Every piece of content — and every content strategy — should pass all six tests before it goes out. If a proposed piece fails more than one, rethink it. If a strategy fails more than one, the niche or mission statement needs work.

---

## 1. Fill a need

The content must answer a genuine, unmet need or question your audience actually has. Not a need you think they should have. Not a question that makes your product look good. An actual question they're asking or a problem they're trying to solve.

Test: Would your audience seek out this information regardless of whether it comes from you? If yes, you're filling a need. If they'd only care because it mentions your brand, you're not.

---

## 2. Be consistent

The hallmark of a publisher is consistency. Irregular content is worse than no content. An audience that subscribes to a weekly newsletter and gets it twice this month and zero times next month learns to ignore you.

Test: Can you sustain this frequency for two years without burning out or running out of ideas? If you can't answer yes confidently, reduce the frequency until you can.

---

## 3. Be human

Content from a brand should not sound like a press release. It should have a voice — a specific personality that readers could identify even if the logo were stripped out. Humor, sarcasm, directness, warmth — pick a tone and maintain it.

Test: Does this sound like a person or a committee? Could you imagine a specific human saying this out loud?

---

## 4. Have a point of view

Epic content takes sides. It makes arguments. It says things that some readers will disagree with. Encyclopedia-style "here are both perspectives" content is forgettable. Your brand has opinions. State them.

Test: Would someone share this because it challenged them or gave them a clear position to agree with? Or is it so neutral that sharing it says nothing about the sharer?

---

## 5. Avoid sales speak

The moment you start talking about your product's features and benefits inside editorial content, you've broken the contract with the reader. They came for value. You're giving them an ad.

Test: Could a direct competitor publish this with their logo instead of yours? If yes, it has no sales speak — and it also has a content tilt problem. Pulizzi's rule: the less you talk about yourself, the more people value your content.

---

## 6. Be best of breed

The goal is not to be pretty good in your niche. The goal is to be the best source of information on this topic for this audience on earth. If you're not aiming for that, your audience has no reason to subscribe to you specifically.

Test: If you asked your target audience to list the five best sources of information on your topic, would they eventually mention you? If not — with enough time and consistency — what would it take to get there?

---

## Using this checklist

When evaluating a strategy or a piece of content, score it against each principle: passes / partial / fails.

Any "fails" is a blocker. Address it before proceeding.
"Partial" on principle 6 is acceptable at launch — you get there over time. Partial on any other principle usually means rewriting.
