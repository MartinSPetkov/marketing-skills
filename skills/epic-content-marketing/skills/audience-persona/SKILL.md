# Audience Persona for Content Marketing

This skill builds a content-focused audience persona — different from a typical buyer persona because it maps what your audience cares about, reads, and wants to learn, not just what they buy.

The core Pulizzi insight: "I am not the target for my content." Business owners and marketers constantly bend content to their own preferences. This skill forces a clear separation between who you are and who you are writing for.

---

## Important distinction: content audience vs. buying audience

These are often different people and both matter.

- A university's content audience is students and parents. The buying decision is made by parents (and sometimes financial aid offices) but the content must appeal to students first.
- A B2B software company's content audience might be individual practitioners (designers, engineers, analysts) while the buying decision is made by their managers.
- A consumer brand's content audience is enthusiasts; the buying audience is anyone with a credit card.

If these audiences differ for this brand, identify both, then confirm which one the content will primarily serve.

---

## Phase 1: Gather raw information

Ask the following questions. Don't accept vague answers — push for specifics.

**Demographics and context:**
- What is this person's job title, role, or life situation?
- What industry or sector are they in?
- What is their experience level in the area your content covers?
- Where do they live and work? (relevant for local/regional content or for understanding context)

**Goals and motivations:**
- What are they trying to accomplish in the next 6–12 months?
- What does success look like for them professionally or personally?
- What would a "great day" look like for this person?

**Pain points and frustrations:**
- What keeps them up at night?
- What problems do they face that your content could help them solve?
- What do they feel stuck on?
- What do they wish they knew but can't find a good source for?

**Content consumption:**
- What newsletters, blogs, podcasts, or YouTube channels do they already rely on?
- What do they read in the morning? What do they listen to commuting?
- How do they prefer to consume content? (text, video, audio, events)
- How much time do they have for content? (5 minutes on a phone vs. 45 minutes with a cup of coffee)

**Trust and authority:**
- Who do they trust for information in this space?
- What makes them trust a source? (credentials, track record, voice, community)
- What makes them distrust a source immediately? (sales tone, jargon, oversimplification)

**The subscription question:**
- What would they need to get from your content to justify giving you their email address?
- What's the one thing your content could deliver that they can't easily find elsewhere?

---

## Phase 2: Identify the informational gap

This is the content tilt applied to the persona. After mapping the persona's existing content diet, find what's missing:

- What topics does the persona care about that no existing source covers well?
- What format does the persona want that doesn't exist yet?
- What level of depth does the persona need that current sources skip (too shallow) or miss (too technical)?

The persona's unmet need IS your content opportunity.

---

## Phase 3: Build the persona document

Produce a persona document with these sections:

```
# Content Audience Persona: [Name]

## At a glance
[3-sentence sketch: who they are, what they're trying to do, what they're frustrated by]

## Demographics and context
[Role, industry, experience level, context]

## Goals
[What they're trying to accomplish; what success looks like]

## Pain points
[What they're stuck on; what they wish existed]

## Current content diet
[What they already read/watch/listen to; what they trust and why]

## Content consumption habits
[How they consume: format, timing, device, attention span]

## The trust test
[What makes them follow a new source; what makes them unsubscribe]

## The subscription question
[What specific promise would make them give you their email address]

## Content gap
[One paragraph: what this persona cannot find anywhere that your content could provide]

## What NOT to do
[Formats, tones, or topics that would immediately lose this person]
```

---

## Phase 4: Validate

Before finalizing, run these tests:

1. **The conference test.** Could you walk into a specific conference and identify 20 people who match this persona? If not, the persona is too broad.
2. **The headline test.** Write three hypothetical newsletter subject lines for this persona. Would a real person with this profile open all three? If you're not sure, the persona lacks enough specificity.
3. **The competitor test.** Does a publication, newsletter, or podcast already serve this exact persona? If yes, that's competitive intelligence — now find the gap within the persona's world that no one owns.

---

## What to do with multiple personas

If the brand has multiple content audiences, pick one for the primary content program. Build secondary personas only after the primary program is working.

Pulizzi's rule: content that tries to serve everyone serves no one. The tighter the persona, the more powerful the content — and paradoxically, the more people outside the persona it ends up attracting because specificity signals credibility.
