# Example Content Audience Persona

**Brand:** SeedRound.io (a platform for early-stage founders)

---

## At a glance

Alex is a 29-year-old first-time founder who just raised a pre-seed round. She's technically strong but has no prior experience running a company. She's excited and terrified in equal measure. Her biggest fear is that she'll make an avoidable mistake that a more experienced founder would have seen coming — and she's burning evenings reading every founder blog she can find trying to prevent that.

---

## Demographics and context

- **Role:** Founder/CEO, pre-seed stage, 2–5 person team
- **Industry:** B2B SaaS, usually some flavor of dev tools, HR tech, or vertical software
- **Experience level:** First-time founder, 5–8 years of work experience prior
- **Context:** Working from home or a co-working space; usually juggling product and sales simultaneously; calendar is chaotic

---

## Goals

- Ship a product that real customers pay for
- Figure out how hiring works before she needs to hire
- Understand enough about fundraising to not get taken advantage of in her next round
- Build processes early enough that the company doesn't fall apart when they're at 15 people

---

## Pain points

- Drowning in conflicting advice — every VC and founder on Twitter says something different
- Can't afford to hire people with relevant experience yet, so she's figuring out HR, finance, and sales cold
- Imposter syndrome: she sees other founders at her stage who seem to have it more together
- She doesn't have a network of founders to ask "is this normal?" in real time

---

## Current content diet

- Lenny's Newsletter (top choice — specific, tactical, written by someone who's done it)
- Y Combinator's resources and essays (trusted but sometimes abstract)
- Indie Hackers threads (useful for morale; less useful for business mechanics)
- Twitter/X founder threads (high noise-to-signal, but she still scrolls)
- First Round Review (good but often about companies at later stages than hers)

**What she trusts:** First-person accounts from founders at her exact stage. Specific numbers, not frameworks. Honest about failure. Not aspirational — practical.

**What she doesn't trust:** VC-written content (suspects an agenda). "10 rules for startup success" listicles. Anyone who has only ever been a founder at 50+ people and claims to understand pre-seed reality.

---

## Content consumption habits

- **Format:** Long-form email newsletter (reads 7–8am with coffee before the day starts); occasional podcast during walks (30-minute max)
- **Device:** Phone for newsletters, laptop for longer reference pieces
- **Attention span:** High for specific, tactical content. Drops off immediately if the opening is vague or abstract.
- **Timing:** She's too busy mid-day. She's reachable in the morning or Sunday evenings.

---

## The trust test

She follows a new source if: it includes specific numbers (actual revenue, specific failure stories, real equity splits), it acknowledges what the author doesn't know, and the first piece she reads gives her something she can act on that week.

She unsubscribes if: the content feels like it was written by a marketing team, there's a pitch in the second email, or the advice is only applicable to VC-backed companies with 50+ employees.

---

## The subscription question

She would give her email for: a bi-weekly "what I wish I'd known in month 6" series written by founders who just passed the 2-year mark — specific mistakes, specific costs, specific things they'd do differently. Not a listicle. An honest debrief.

---

## Content gap

The founder content market is enormous but almost entirely bifurcated: there's aspirational content written by successful founders looking back from Series B, and there's abstract VC content aimed at portfolio companies. Almost nothing is written in real time, at the pre-seed to seed stage, with the specificity of Lenny's Newsletter but focused on operational mechanics rather than product. Alex has no trusted source that speaks to hiring your first 3 people when you have $800k in the bank, or how to structure a co-founder agreement before things go wrong.

---

## What NOT to do

- No motivational content ("you've got this!"). She's past needing permission.
- No frameworks without examples. She has too many frameworks already.
- No content that assumes she has an operations team or a chief of staff.
- No content written for product managers that's been relabeled "for founders."
