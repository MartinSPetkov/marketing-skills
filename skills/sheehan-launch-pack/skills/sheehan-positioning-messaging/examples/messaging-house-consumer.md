# Example messaging house: SnapFit (consumer fitness app)

Full messaging house for a hypothetical mobile fitness app launch.

## Positioning statement

For busy parents who want to stay in shape but can't carve out 45 minutes for a gym, SnapFit is a workout app that delivers a 12-minute workout you can do at home with no equipment. Unlike Peloton and Apple Fitness+, our product is built for 12-minute windows between dropping kids off and getting on calls.

## Tagline

12 minutes. No equipment. Real results.

## Pillar 1: Built for 12 minutes

**Subhead:** Every workout is exactly 12 minutes. Start when you sit down. Finish before the next thing.

**Proof:**

- Every workout timed to the second: 2 min warmup, 8 min main set, 2 min cooldown.
- 400+ workouts, all 12 minutes.
- Customer quote: "I finally stopped feeling guilty about not working out. I just do 12 minutes." (App Store review)
- Compare: Peloton classes average 30-45 minutes.

## Pillar 2: No equipment, no excuses

**Subhead:** Just your body, a patch of floor, and a phone. Works in a hotel, a bedroom, a park.

**Proof:**

- Zero equipment required across the full library.
- Bedroom-friendly modifications for low-impact workouts.
- Customer quote: "Did a workout in the hallway at a work trip. Nobody needed to know." (App Store review)
- Compare: Peloton Bike is $1,500 + subscription; Apple Fitness+ requires Apple Watch.

## Pillar 3: Proof it works

**Subhead:** Real-user data on weight loss, strength gains, and consistency. Not before-and-after marketing photos.

**Proof:**

- 65% of users complete 4+ workouts per week in month 2, versus 18% industry average for fitness apps.
- Independent study: average user lost 4.2 lbs and gained 9% strength in 90 days.
- Trainer team: 12 NASM-certified coaches, average 8 years experience.
- Customer quote: "It's the first app I've used more than 3 months." (App Store review)

## Persona variants

### Primary persona: Parent of kids under 10

**Opening pain:** "I used to go to the gym. I haven't been in 3 years. I don't even fit my workout clothes anymore."
**Lead message:** Pillar 1 (12 minutes).
**Proof priority:** Time; testimonials from other parents.

### Secondary persona: Busy knowledge worker, no kids

**Opening pain:** "My calendar is full. Working out is the first thing that falls off when I'm slammed."
**Lead message:** Pillar 2 (no equipment, anywhere).
**Proof priority:** Hotel and travel-friendly; no setup required.

### Tertiary persona: Comeback user (used a fitness app before, fell off)

**Opening pain:** "I paid for Peloton for a year and used it 6 times. Every app I try, I quit in 2 months."
**Lead message:** Pillar 3 (proof it works, retention data).
**Proof priority:** Consistency stats, long-term user quotes.

## Language notes

- **Use:** "12 minutes" (specific, memorable), "your floor, your phone" (tangible), "no excuses" (empathetic, not shaming).
- **Avoid:** "transform your body," "fitness journey," "level up," "unleash your potential." All generic fitness marketing.

## Competitive watch-outs

- **Don't claim:** "Better than Peloton." It's not. It's built for a different use case.
- **Own:** "Fits your life, not the other way around."
- **Neutralize:** Objection "but 12 minutes isn't enough for real results." Response: "Independent study shows 4.2 lbs lost and 9% strength gain in 90 days on 12-minute daily workouts. Consistency beats duration."

## App Store headline (from the house)

**Subtitle:** No equipment. No gym. Real results.
