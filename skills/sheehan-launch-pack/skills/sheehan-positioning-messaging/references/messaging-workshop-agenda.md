# Messaging workshop: 90-minute agenda

Gather inputs for positioning and messaging before writing. One 90-minute session beats three rounds of comment-ping-pong on a document.

## Who to invite

4-8 people:

- PMM (facilitator, note-taker, usually the launch lead).
- PM (product POV).
- Sales lead (buyer objections, what closes deals).
- CS lead (what customers actually value after buying).
- Executive sponsor (CMO or CEO for alignment and sign-off).
- Optional: a senior AE who closes a lot of the target segment.

Less than 4 is too narrow. More than 8 and it becomes a debate, not a working session.

## Pre-reads (sent 48 hours ahead)

- Target market and persona document.
- Competitive alternatives list.
- Any JTBD interview highlights or customer quote bank.
- Current positioning (if one exists).

## Agenda

### 0-10 min: Review target market (10 min)

Facilitator walks through the primary persona. Who they are, what job they're doing, what they've tried before.

No debate here. If the room disagrees with the persona, stop the workshop and fix the persona first.

### 10-20 min: Review competitive alternatives (10 min)

List every real alternative, including doing nothing and homegrown workarounds. Pick the top 1-2 as the "primary" competitors.

### 20-40 min: Brainstorm what matters most (20 min)

Prompt: "If our customer could only say one thing about why they chose us, what would they say?"

Structure:

- 5 min silent writing on index cards or in a shared doc. One answer per card.
- 10 min sharing, grouping by theme.
- 5 min voting: everyone picks their top 3 with sticky dots.

Output: 3-5 clusters of candidate pillars.

### 40-60 min: Pressure-test (20 min)

For each cluster, answer:

1. Is it true? Can we prove it today, not in 6 months?
2. Is it different? Does the top competitor claim the same thing?
3. Does it matter? Did customers mention this pain in interviews?
4. Can we say it simply? 3-7 word headline possible?

Kill clusters that fail 2 of 4. What survives is the pillar short list.

### 60-80 min: Draft the positioning statement (20 min)

Use the template:

> For [target] who [need], [product] is a [category]
> that [key benefit]. Unlike [competitor],
> our product [differentiation].

Work it live on a whiteboard or shared doc. One blank at a time. No polishing, just capturing decisions.

### 80-90 min: Assign next steps (10 min)

- PMM writes the first draft of the messaging house within 48 hours.
- Attendees get 3 business days to comment.
- Exec sponsor signs off within 1 week.
- Target final version: 10 business days from workshop.

## Pre-workshop questionnaire (optional, send ahead)

For each attendee, 5 questions:

1. In one sentence, who is this product for?
2. What's the biggest pain point they have today?
3. What's the one thing our product does that no alternative does as well?
4. What's a direct quote from a customer that captures why they bought?
5. What's the most common objection we hear? How do we answer it?

Collect responses before the workshop. Patterns and disagreements will jump out, and the workshop starts with real inputs instead of cold-room brainstorming.

## Facilitator rules

- Kill tangents fast. "Good point, we'll cover that in pillar 2. Back to X."
- Force decisions in the room. "We'll decide later" means someone has to recreate context on a Friday.
- Write everything on the shared doc live. No "I'll type up my notes." Decisions captured in real time hold.
- Don't let the exec voice drown the rest. Especially watch for the CEO rewriting pillars mid-session.

## What to avoid

- Don't run this before you have target market data. You'll write positioning for the customer you imagine, not the one you have.
- Don't let it run past 90 minutes. If you haven't reached a draft statement by minute 80, the underlying strategy isn't clear enough; take it offline.
- Don't skip the signoff. Positioning without exec agreement becomes a moving target for every subsequent piece of copy.
