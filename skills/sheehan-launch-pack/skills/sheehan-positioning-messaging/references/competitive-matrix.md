# Competitive matrix

A 2-axis grid: dimensions down, competitors across. Internal artifact. Don't publish externally.

## Why build one

- Force honest comparison. Your product is not better at everything.
- Find real differentiation, not imagined.
- Give sales the facts for competitive deals.
- Inform pricing and roadmap.

## The structure

```
| Dimension | Us | Competitor A | Competitor B | Status Quo (manual) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Job A: speed | ★★★★ | ★★★ | ★★★★★ | ★ |
| Job B: ease | ★★★★★ | ★★★ | ★★ | N/A |
| Feature X | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Feature Y | Yes | No | No | No |
| Price | $$ | $$$ | $ | Free |
| Support quality | High | Medium | Medium | N/A |
| Implementation time | 1 day | 2 weeks | 1 week | Days of setup |
| Target segment | SMB | Enterprise | SMB | Any |
```

## What to include as dimensions

- The 3-5 jobs the customer is hiring the product for (from JTBD research).
- Major features customers care about (from survey or interview data).
- Price, packaging, and plan structure.
- Implementation and time to value.
- Support and service.
- Ecosystem (integrations, partners).
- Target segment and ideal customer.

Don't include every feature your product has. 8-12 dimensions is the range; more and it becomes noise.

## What to include as columns

- Your product.
- The top 2-3 direct competitors.
- The status quo: what customers do if they don't buy anyone's product. Often the real competitor.
- Optionally: a "category king" for context.

## Scoring rules

- Use stars (1-5) or Yes/No. Don't mix vague words like "strong" and "limited."
- Score based on customer perception, not internal view. What customers say in reviews, not what product thinks is true.
- If you're not sure how a competitor scores, leave it blank and mark "research needed." Don't guess.
- Score yourself honestly. Overrating yourself on the matrix means sales gets caught off-guard in deals.

## Sources for scoring

- G2, Capterra, TrustRadius reviews.
- Gartner, Forrester, IDC reports (where applicable).
- Win/loss interviews.
- Competitor websites (pricing, feature pages).
- Product demo recordings from sales calls.
- Customer feedback, support tickets.

Cite sources in a notes column. An unsourced score is a guess.

## Using the matrix

- For positioning: find 1-2 dimensions where you win clearly. Those become differentiators.
- For sales: build a one-page battle card per competitor from the relevant rows.
- For roadmap: identify gaps where you lose badly on dimensions customers care about.
- For pricing: place yourself relative to competitors on the price/value axis.

## What not to do

- Don't publish the matrix externally. Competitors will dispute it, customers will distrust it, and legal may object.
- Don't update it once and forget. Refresh every 6 months; competitive state moves.
- Don't overrate yourself. Sales will find out in week 2 when a prospect quotes a G2 review back.
- Don't include features nobody asked about. They clutter the matrix and steer attention to the wrong fight.

## Version control

- Store in the launch folder.
- PMM owns updates.
- Sales, PM, and CS can suggest edits but not change scores without PMM sign-off.
- Date-stamp each version. "Q3 2026 competitive matrix" beats "competitive matrix final FINAL v2."
