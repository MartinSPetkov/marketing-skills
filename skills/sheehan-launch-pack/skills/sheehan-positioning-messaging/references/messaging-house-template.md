# Messaging house template

One page. Three levels. Every external piece of copy on the launch draws from here.

## The template

```
# Messaging house: [Product name]

## Tagline
[5-10 word external statement. Sits on the homepage hero.]

## Pillar 1: [Headline, 3-7 words]
**Subhead:** [1-2 sentences. What this pillar means for the customer.]
**Proof:**
- [Feature, data point, customer quote]
- [Feature, data point, customer quote]
- [Feature, data point, customer quote]

## Pillar 2: [Headline]
**Subhead:** ...
**Proof:**
- ...

## Pillar 3: [Headline]
**Subhead:** ...
**Proof:**
- ...

## Persona variants

### Primary persona: [Name]
**Opening pain:** [The pain point in their words]
**Lead message:** [Which pillar leads for this persona]
**Proof priority:** [Which proofs matter most]

### Secondary persona: [Name]
**Opening pain:** ...
**Lead message:** ...
**Proof priority:** ...

## Language notes
- Use: [words the customer actually says]
- Avoid: [words that didn't land in testing or sound internal]

## Competitive watch-outs
- Don't claim: [things a competitor will contest]
- Own: [areas of real, defensible difference]
```

## Rules for filling it in

### Pillars

- 3 is default. 2 is fine. 4 is the ceiling.
- Each pillar expresses one idea that matters to the customer.
- Headlines: 3-7 words. Benefits, not features. "Close the books in a day" beats "Automated GL reconciliation."
- Subheads: 1-2 sentences max. If you need a paragraph, the idea is too fuzzy.

### Proof

- Every claim has at least one proof point. No exceptions.
- Prefer data and quotes over feature names. "Customers cut month-end from 5 days to 1" beats "Automated GL, AP reconciliation, and FX module."
- Mix types: a stat, a quote, a feature, a comparison. Readers believe different things.

### Persona variants

- If there's one persona, skip this section.
- If there are 2-3 personas with different pains, write a short variant for each.
- The same pillars still hold across personas; only emphasis and language shift.

### Language notes

- Populate this from JTBD interviews.
- Words to use: phrases customers repeated in interviews.
- Words to avoid: internal terms, competitor language, jargon that tested poorly.

## What good looks like

- Fits on one page when printed.
- A new hire can read it and write a decent homepage headline.
- Sales can recite the 3 pillars after one training session.
- Every launch asset (blog, email, deck) can be traced back to a specific row.

## What bad looks like

- 8 pillars, each a paragraph long.
- Pillars that are synonyms ("We're fast" and "We're quick").
- Proof points that are restatements of the claim.
- Persona variants that are identical with the name swapped.
- Language that the team uses but customers don't.

## Refresh cadence

Revisit every 6-12 months, or when:

- A major competitive move forces repositioning.
- Customer research shows the pain language has shifted.
- Sales reports pillar X stops landing in demos.
- The product has changed enough that the pillars don't cover what it now does.

Don't refresh because it's "time." Refresh because the evidence demands it.
