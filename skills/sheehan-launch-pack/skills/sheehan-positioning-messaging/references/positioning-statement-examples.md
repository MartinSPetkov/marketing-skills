# Positioning statement examples

Five filled-out statements across industries. Each shows the pattern and the reasoning.

## Example 1: B2B SaaS (product analytics)

> For Heads of Product at Series B-D B2B SaaS companies who need to see how users move through their product but don't have a dedicated data team, Acme Insights is a product analytics tool that installs in 30 minutes and answers the 5 questions every PM asks on day one. Unlike Mixpanel and Amplitude, our product is priced for teams under 50 and doesn't require a data engineer to maintain.

**Why it works:**

- Specific target: Series B-D, B2B SaaS, no data team.
- Specific need: 5 questions every PM asks on day one.
- Specific category: product analytics (not "analytics platform," which is vague).
- Specific benefit: 30-minute install, PM-friendly.
- Specific differentiator: pricing and no-data-engineer-required.

## Example 2: Consumer mobile app (fitness)

> For busy parents who want to stay in shape but can't carve out 45 minutes for a gym, SnapFit is a workout app that delivers a 12-minute workout you can do at home with no equipment. Unlike Peloton and Apple Fitness+, our product is built for 12-minute windows between dropping kids off and getting on calls.

**Why it works:**

- Honest about the constraint (time, not motivation).
- Specific duration: 12 minutes, not "quick workouts."
- Specific use case: between kids and calls.
- Clear differentiator against named, well-known competitors.

## Example 3: Developer tool (API platform)

> For backend engineers at fast-growing startups who are tired of building and maintaining authentication from scratch, AuthCore is an authentication API that handles login, OAuth, SSO, and MFA with 4 lines of code. Unlike Auth0 and Clerk, our product bills on active users only and open-sources the client libraries.

**Why it works:**

- Target is specific: backend engineers, fast-growing startups.
- Need is emotional and functional: tired of building from scratch.
- Benefit is measurable: 4 lines of code.
- Differentiation is business-model, not just technical: pricing and open-source.

## Example 4: Internal tool (for an existing customer base)

> For HR leaders at companies already using Workday who spend hours a week chasing managers for approvals, FlowHR is a Slack-native approval layer that cuts manager-chase time by 70%. Unlike Workday's native notifications, our product meets managers where they already are and delivers mobile-friendly approvals without opening a second tab.

**Why it works:**

- Narrow target: HR leaders at Workday customers.
- Specific problem: chasing managers for approvals.
- Measurable benefit.
- Differentiation is context (Slack-native), not a feature war with Workday.

## Example 5: Reposition (not a new launch, but a reframe)

> For revenue operations leaders at B2B SaaS companies that run on Salesforce and find themselves writing flows and triggers every time the go-to-market motion changes, RevMotion is a no-code workflow builder for GTM teams. Unlike Zapier and Workato, our product is built specifically around Salesforce objects and GTM workflows, so it speaks your team's language out of the box.

**Why it works:**

- Reposition from "general workflow tool" to "GTM workflow tool for Salesforce shops."
- Narrower market, more specific pain, clearer differentiation.
- Status quo (custom flows) named as the real alternative.

## Patterns across all five

- **Specific target.** Every statement names a role and a company type, not a generic audience.
- **Honest competitor.** The real alternative, even if it's uncomfortable (a bigger company, the status quo, or a free tool).
- **Provable differentiator.** Numbers, business model, or context. Not "better UX."
- **No jargon.** Every word would make sense to a customer.

## Common failures

- Too broad: "For anyone who wants to grow their business..." Delete and restart.
- Feature-as-benefit: "that has 24/7 support and 99.9% uptime." Those are features. The benefit is what they allow.
- Vague differentiation: "Unlike our competitors, we're easier to use." Prove it or cut it.
- Imagined target: "For Series A founders." If you haven't sold to a Series A founder, you're guessing.
