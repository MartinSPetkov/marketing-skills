# Example: two-week email survey plan

A worked plan for when a PMM has 2 weeks, a customer list of 5,000, and needs to validate a target market hypothesis before finalizing positioning.

## Context

- Product: New "team plan" tier for an existing B2B SaaS.
- Launch: 6 weeks out.
- Hypothesis: "The team plan is for Engineering Managers at 50-500 person SaaS companies who currently pay for a bunch of individual seats and want consolidated billing and admin controls."
- Gap: no one has talked to an Engineering Manager about this. The assumption came from sales anecdotes.

## Week 1

### Day 1-2: Build the survey

10 questions in TypeForm.

1. What is your role? [Engineering Manager / IC Engineer / Director / VP / Other]
2. How many people are on your team? [1-5 / 6-15 / 16-50 / 50+]
3. Who currently pays for your [product] subscription? [Me personally / My company, paid as individual seats / My company, on a team plan / I'm not sure]
4. How many [product] seats does your company have today? [1-5 / 6-20 / 21-50 / 51+ / Not sure]
5. Which of these are pain points for you today? [Multiple choice: billing multiple seats, admin visibility, seat management, SSO, none of these]
6. If a consolidated team plan existed at [price range], would you or your manager consider switching? [Likely / Maybe / Unlikely / Not my decision]
7. Who would make the final decision on a plan like that? [Me / My manager / Finance / IT / Not sure]
8. How would you prefer to evaluate a new plan? [Free trial / Sales call / Self-serve upgrade / Other]
9. What would stop you from upgrading? [Open text]
10. Can we follow up with a 20-minute call for a $75 gift card? [Yes + email / No]

### Day 3: Segment the list

Pull 2 segments from the customer database:

- Segment A: Accounts with 3+ paid individual seats and at least 1 Engineering Manager as a user. Estimated 800 contacts.
- Segment B: Accounts with 1-2 seats at companies with 50+ employees. Estimated 1,200 contacts.

### Day 4: Send

Send from the PMM's email, not a noreply address. Subject line test: "Quick question about how you use [product]" vs. "2 minutes, $10 Amazon card."

Winner after 24 hours: the direct subject line. Scale it out.

### Day 5-7: Collect responses

Target: 200 responses across both segments. Check response rate daily. Send one reminder on Day 6.

## Week 2

### Day 8-9: Analyze

Look for:

- Which role reports the pain points most? If Engineering Managers don't feel the billing pain but Finance does, the hypothesis is wrong about who the buyer is.
- Which segment is most interested? If Segment B is twice as likely to upgrade, that shifts the launch target.
- What's the actual decision process? If most say "Finance decides," the launch needs a Finance-facing message, not just an Engineering one.

### Day 10-11: Run 5 follow-up interviews

From the 40+ people who said yes to a follow-up call, pick 5 covering a mix of roles and company sizes. 20 minutes each. Use a short version of the JTBD script, focused on the buying process.

### Day 12-13: Synthesize

Build 1-2 personas. Compare to the hypothesis. If the primary buyer is Finance or IT (not Engineering Management), rewrite the positioning before it hard-sets.

### Day 14: Share back

Walk the product, sales, and marketing leads through findings in a 30-minute meeting. Decisions to make:

- Who is the primary persona?
- Does the pricing tier match what they'd pay?
- What's the sales motion: self-serve, sales-assist, or full sales?

## Budget

- $75 per follow-up interview x 5 = $375.
- $10 gift cards for 200 survey completions = $2,000.
- TypeForm: $35/month.
- Total: ~$2,400.

## What you learn that you wouldn't have learned otherwise

- Who actually has the pain (may not be who sales thinks).
- Whether price is a yes/no decision or a negotiation.
- Real words to use in the launch copy.
- Realistic conversion rate, which feeds the goal math.
