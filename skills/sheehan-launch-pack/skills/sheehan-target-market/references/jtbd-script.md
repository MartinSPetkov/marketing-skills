# Jobs to Be Done interview script

Adapted from Clayton Christensen's Jobs to Be Done methodology as used by Mary Sheehan. Use the same script for every interview so answers compare.

## Before the call

- Get written consent to record.
- Budget 30 minutes. Cut to 20 if the interviewee is short on time; never go over 45.
- Have the recording software ready before they join. Test audio.
- Open with a thank-you and a short intro of who you are and why you're asking. Do not pitch the product.

## The script

### 1. First touch

"Think back to when you first started looking for a solution like this. What was going on in your life or work at that time?"

Probe for:

- Trigger event. Something broke, changed, or became urgent.
- Emotional state. Were they frustrated, curious, scared, bored?
- Who else was involved.

### 2. Consideration set

"What options did you consider? Walk me through everything you looked at, even the ones you ruled out quickly."

Probe for:

- Competitors by name.
- Homegrown workarounds (spreadsheets, scripts, manual processes).
- Doing nothing.
- How they found each option.

### 3. Decision criteria

"How did you decide between them? What made one option better than another?"

Probe for:

- Features they cared about.
- Price sensitivity.
- Trust signals (reviews, referrals, analyst reports).
- Time pressure.

### 4. The buy moment

"Walk me through the actual moment of buying or starting to use it. Who was involved? What was the approval process like?"

Probe for:

- Budget source.
- Approvers and influencers.
- Contract or procurement pain.
- How long from first touch to first use.

### 5. First use

"Tell me about the first week using it. What worked, what didn't?"

Probe for:

- Setup friction.
- First win (the 'aha' moment).
- Anything they almost gave up on.

### 6. Ongoing use

"How do you use it now? What would make you stop using it?"

Probe for:

- Habits formed.
- Churn triggers.
- Adjacent jobs they'd want solved.

### 7. The bigger picture

"Step back. What job were you really trying to get done when you went looking for this? Not the product features, but the thing you were actually trying to accomplish."

This is the Job to Be Done. Listen for functional, emotional, and social components.

### 8. Referral

"Who else should I talk to about this?"

## After the call

- Within 24 hours, write a 1-page summary: trigger, consideration set, decision moment, job, pain points, exact quotes.
- Tag quotes by theme as you go. Patterns show up around interview 5-7.
- Flag the best quotes for use in messaging later.

## Rules of the road

- Ask open questions. "Tell me about..." beats "Did you...?"
- Silence is fine. Let them think.
- Do not correct them or defend the product.
- Do not lead. "Was price the main factor?" is a leading question. Ask "What were the factors?" instead.
- If you catch yourself explaining the product, stop.
