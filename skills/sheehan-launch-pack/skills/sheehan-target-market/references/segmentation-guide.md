# Segmentation guide

How to find segments in the research and when to stop splitting.

## Why segment

"It's for everyone" breaks launches because it breaks channel selection, messaging, and sales process. Segmenting forces the team to pick where to win first.

Two to four segments is typical for a launch. More than four usually means the hypothesis was too broad.

## Axes to segment on

Try these axes, in order of usefulness for a launch:

1. **Job to be done.** Different jobs, different segments. A CFO who wants "fewer month-end fire drills" is not the same segment as a controller who wants "a faster monthly close," even if they buy the same product.
2. **Buying process.** Self-serve versus sales-led buyers behave differently. Segment them.
3. **Company stage or size.** Startup, mid-market, enterprise. Different motions, different decision makers.
4. **Industry.** Only split by industry if the job or buying process differs. Don't split just because sales asks you to.
5. **Behavior today.** What tools and workarounds they use now. Often the strongest predictor of fit.
6. **Geography.** Only if channels, regulations, or language differ.

Avoid segmenting on demographics alone (age, gender, income). Those rarely predict behavior well in B2B, and they make the persona vague.

## How to find segments in the research

After 15-25 interviews, do a pattern pass:

1. Pull every interview summary into one doc.
2. Tag each by: trigger, job to be done, alternatives considered, buying process, pain points, channels.
3. Sort and cluster. Do 3-5 clusters emerge where people sound like each other? Those are candidate segments.
4. Name each cluster by its defining trait. "The Overloaded Controller" beats "Cluster 2."
5. Sanity check: does each cluster have a distinct channel plan and messaging angle? If two clusters look the same on paper, merge them.

## When to stop splitting

Stop when one more segment would not change the launch plan. If two segments share the same channels, the same messaging, and the same sales process, they are not actually separate segments for this launch.

Signs you've split too much:

- Each persona reads like a rewording of the last.
- Channels and messaging are identical across segments.
- Sales can't remember which persona to pitch.

Signs you haven't split enough:

- One persona's pain points contradict another's.
- Must-haves differ by a factor of 2 in count.
- Channels list is 10+ items because "we have to be everywhere."

## Prioritizing segments for the launch

A launch can target one segment well, two segments with compromise, or three segments badly. Pick the primary segment using these filters:

- **Size.** Big enough to hit the launch goals? Do the math.
- **Access.** Can we reach them through channels we can afford?
- **Urgency.** Do they have a burning job we can show up for?
- **Fit.** Does the product actually solve their job better than alternatives?
- **Expansion.** Does winning this segment open doors to the next?

If two segments tie, pick the one with the clearest urgency. Urgent buyers close faster and give better case studies.

## Secondary segments

Name them explicitly but don't build the launch around them. "We'll also reach X and Y through the content, but the GTM motion is built for the primary." This keeps the team honest when sales wants to chase a shiny second segment on week 2.
