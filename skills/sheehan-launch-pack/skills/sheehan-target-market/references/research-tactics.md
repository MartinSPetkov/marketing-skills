# Research tactics in detail

Six tactics for getting launch-ready customer research done in weeks, not quarters. Pick one or combine two.

## 1. Email surveys

**Fastest path to volume.** Good when you have a customer or prospect list of at least a few hundred.

- Tools: TypeForm, Google Forms, SurveyMonkey.
- Keep it under 10 questions. Mix multiple choice with 2-3 open-ended.
- Segment the list. Send different versions to current customers, churned customers, and prospects.
- Offer a $5-10 gift card for completion. Raffle a bigger prize ($250-500) for a pool of respondents.
- Expect 5-15% response rate. Under 5% means the subject line or incentive is off.

**When it fails:** surveys tell you what people think, not what they do. Don't use them alone for deep motivation research.

## 2. Phone or video interviews

**Highest signal per hour.** Best for understanding motivation and language.

- 30 minutes, Zoom or phone.
- At least 5 interviews; under 5 and a single outlier distorts the sample. For segmentation work, target 25-30.
- Use Calendly for scheduling. Use Zoom, Dialpad, or Fireflies for recording. Get consent (California and other two-party-consent states require it).
- Same JTBD script for everyone. See `jtbd-script.md`.
- Offer $50-100 for a 30-minute interview.

**When it fails:** if you outsource all of them, nobody on the team hears the customer's language. Always run 3-5 yourself.

## 3. iPad at a conference or virtual booth

**Target audience concentrated in one room.** Works at trade shows or industry events.

- Load a short survey (5 questions max) on an iPad.
- Offer a branded freebie (socks, stickers, coffee voucher).
- Aim for 50-100 responses over 2 days.
- Great for quick demographic and behavior data, weaker for motivation.

**When it fails:** sample skews toward people who attend conferences. Cross-check with other sources.

## 4. LinkedIn direct messages

**Best for recruiting when no list exists.** Slow but targeted.

- Write 3-4 headline variants. Test which gets the best response rate.
- Message the exact persona you're after (Head of Product at Series B SaaS, Ops Manager at $100M-$500M retailer, etc.).
- Offer a short incentive ($50 for 20 minutes) or frame as "We're building something that might help you, want to show it to you and get your reaction."
- Expect 5-10% reply rate on cold DMs.

**When it fails:** you end up talking to people who have time on LinkedIn, not your actual buyers.

## 5. Recruitment tools (Vancery, User Interviews, Respondent)

**Fast and precise but expensive.** $100-500 per respondent depending on seniority.

- Good for hard-to-reach personas (C-suite, regulated industries).
- Screening questions matter; bad screens mean you pay for the wrong people.
- Lead time: 3-5 days from brief to first interview.

**When it fails:** panel participants are paid to answer questions. They'll give you polished answers. Look for hesitation, confusion, emotion, not just clean soundbites.

## 6. Incentives across the board

Rough benchmarks for getting people to show up:

| Ask | Incentive |
|---|---|
| 5-minute survey | $5-10 gift card |
| 15-minute survey | $15-25 gift card |
| 30-minute interview | $50-100 |
| 60-minute interview | $100-200 |
| Larger study sweepstakes | $250-500 raffle |

For B2B executives, donations to charity often work better than gift cards. For existing customers, public recognition (quote on the website, case study) sometimes replaces cash.

## Picking the tactic

| Timeline | List status | Budget | Tactic |
|---|---|---|---|
| <2 weeks | No list | Any | LinkedIn + Vancery |
| 2-4 weeks | Small list | Medium | Email survey + 5 interviews |
| 2-4 weeks | Large list | Medium | Email survey + 10 interviews |
| 1-2 months | Any | Higher | 25-30 JTBD interviews |
| Event coming | N/A | Low | iPad at booth |

Combine two whenever possible. Surveys tell you size; interviews tell you why.
