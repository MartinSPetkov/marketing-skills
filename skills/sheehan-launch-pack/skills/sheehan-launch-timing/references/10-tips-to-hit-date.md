# 10 tips to hit your launch date

Field-tested rules adapted from Mary Sheehan's book. Each one prevents a specific kind of slip.

## 1. Lock the tier early

**Why:** Tier debates (Is this Tier 1 or Tier 2? Do we need PR? Do we need a press tour?) eat weeks of calendar time when they happen late.

**Rule:** Tier signed off by exec team at T-12 weeks for Tier 1, T-6 for Tier 2. If tier changes later, the plan is effectively starting over.

## 2. Name the launch lead early

**Why:** Launches without a single named owner drift. Two people thinking "someone's got this" means nobody has it.

**Rule:** Name the launch lead at kickoff. Write their name in the GTM plan. Publish it.

## 3. Hold a dress rehearsal

**Why:** Dress rehearsals at T-5 days find broken things cheaply. Without one, you find them at T+30 minutes, live.

**Rule:** 90-minute call, core team, walk the entire launch day checklist in real time. Push to staging. Test every link. Expect 3-5 findings.

## 4. Set a messaging no-change date

**Why:** "One more round of feedback" at T-1 week destroys launches. Someone rewrites the headline. Copy chain reacts everywhere.

**Rule:** T-3 weeks is the no-change date for Tier 1. After that, exec sign-off required to change any messaging. After T-1 week, nothing changes.

## 5. Certify sales reps by T-2 weeks

**Why:** Reps who can't pitch don't pitch. Or worse, they pitch wrong and confuse prospects.

**Rule:** Record every rep's pitch. 90%+ certified by T-2 weeks. No pitch, no lead assignment.

## 6. Build slack into the schedule

**Why:** Everything takes longer than planned. If the schedule is tight end-to-end, one slip cascades.

**Rule:** Add 2-3 buffer days in the middle of the schedule. Don't pretend they're not there; they're there to absorb inevitable slips without moving the launch date.

## 7. Run weekly syncs with green/yellow/red

**Why:** Status-dump meetings hide problems. Explicit color-coded readouts surface them.

**Rule:** Every weekly sync, the PMM asks each section owner: green, yellow, or red? Yellow or red two weeks in a row = escalate to exec sponsor.

## 8. Agree go/no-go criteria in writing at T-8 weeks

**Why:** Without written criteria, go/no-go at T-1 week becomes a vibes discussion with a shipping deadline.

**Rule:** At T-8 weeks, write the criteria. Product quality bar, sales readiness, press embargo, tracking live, creative reviewed. Exec sponsor signs.

## 9. Publish the launch date early

**Why:** Once press agencies have told reporters about the embargo date, moving the date has a cost. Use that.

**Rule:** Tell press (under embargo) at T-4 to T-6 weeks. The external commitment turns into a forcing function.

## 10. Write the day-of runbook two weeks before launch

**Why:** Writing the runbook on launch day morning is how details get missed.

**Rule:** Runbook drafted at T-3 weeks, reviewed at T-2, finalized at T-1 week. Read it aloud in the dress rehearsal.

## Bonus: common date killers and how to survive them

**Engineering slip.** One feature isn't going to make it. Options: cut the feature and launch on time (often best), reduce scope and launch, or slip the date. Never slip without writing down why, because that cause tends to repeat.

**Legal review.** Press releases and blog posts go through legal. Factor in 3-5 business days. Don't send the night before.

**Exec cold feet.** "I want to see one more version of the messaging" at T-2 weeks. Firm response: "Here's the exec sign-off from week 8. Any changes need written exec approval and a new sign-off."

**Competitor launches near you.** If a competitor launches in your window, don't panic-move. Check whether the stories will conflate or contrast. If contrast, stay put. If conflate, move 2-3 weeks.

**External news (macroeconomic, political, cultural).** Major news can crush press coverage. If a launch lands on a national tragedy or election day, the press will ignore it. Move if you can; post a short note and soft-launch if you can't.

**PR agency misses.** Press coverage under-delivers. Don't slip the date; adjust the rolling thunder plan to compensate post-launch.

## When to actually move the date

Slip the date if:

- Sev-1 product issue with no workaround.
- A foundational piece of positioning turns out to be wrong (usually surfaces at T-4 to T-2 weeks).
- An exec sponsor withdraws and there's no replacement.

Don't slip the date for:

- One feature that's not quite ready.
- Marketing copy that's not perfect.
- A competitor's recent news.

If you do slip, write up why, share with the team, and update the process to prevent the same cause next time.
