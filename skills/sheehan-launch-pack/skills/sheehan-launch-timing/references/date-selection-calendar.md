# Date selection calendar

Big events, holidays, and press blackout windows. Updated periodically; check current-year dates.

## Dates to avoid (US-focused)

### Holiday windows (low press attention, thin staffing)

- **New Year's Eve / Day.** Dec 30 - Jan 3.
- **Martin Luther King Jr. Day.** Third Monday of January.
- **Presidents' Day.** Third Monday of February.
- **Memorial Day.** Last Monday of May (and the week leading up).
- **Independence Day.** July 4th and the full week around it (July 1-8).
- **Labor Day.** First Monday of September.
- **Thanksgiving week.** Monday-Friday of the Thanksgiving week. Also the Monday after.
- **Christmas and end-of-year.** Dec 20 - Jan 1.

### Industry events (unless you're there)

- **CES.** Early January, Las Vegas. Tech press is swamped.
- **RSA Conference.** Late April / early May, San Francisco. Security press is swamped.
- **Google I/O.** May, Mountain View. Developer press is consumed.
- **WWDC.** June, San Jose. Apple dominates press.
- **Dreamforce.** Mid-September, San Francisco. Salesforce-related stories dominate.
- **AWS re:Invent.** Late November / early December, Las Vegas. Cloud press is swamped.
- **Gartner Symposium/ITxpo.** October, Orlando. IT press is focused there.
- **SXSW.** Mid-March, Austin. Consumer and media press is focused there.
- **Cannes Lions.** Mid-June, France. Ad industry press is focused there.
- **Art Basel, CES Asia, etc.** Region-specific.

### Press blackout windows

- **Public company earnings weeks.** Late January, late April, late July, late October for many US public companies. Reporters are covering earnings calls.
- **National elections.** First Tuesday after the first Monday in November (even-year Novembers). Political news dominates.

## Dates to aim for

### Strong launch weeks

- **Mid-February.** Back from holidays, no major events.
- **Mid-March** (avoid SXSW week).
- **Mid-April** (avoid RSA if you're in security).
- **Early-to-mid May.**
- **Mid-September** (before Dreamforce).
- **Mid-to-late October** (avoid Gartner if you're in IT).
- **Early November** (before election if applicable, before Thanksgiving).

### Day of the week

- **Tuesday.** Best. Reporters have the week ahead for coverage.
- **Wednesday.** Second-best.
- **Thursday.** OK.
- **Monday.** Inbox backlog. Avoid.
- **Friday.** Press attention drops by Saturday. Avoid unless you're targeting a weekend-friendly audience.

### Time of day

- **7-8am PT / 10-11am ET.** Hits morning news cycles on both coasts.
- **Avoid after 2pm ET.** Coverage window is short.

## Customer event alignment

If you run your own conference or user summit, launching there amplifies. Common patterns:

- Keynote launch: major product news breaks during the CEO keynote.
- Pre-conference launch: announce 1-2 weeks before so the conference becomes the discussion forum.
- Post-conference launch: announce 2-3 weeks after if the conference is more about community than news.

If a major trade show is in your space and you're exhibiting, tie the launch to day 1 of the show.

## Cross-checks before locking a date

- Is anyone at your company launching something else within 2 weeks of this date?
- Is a major competitor announcing in this window?
- Is there a known industry event or moment (regulation, big M&A) expected in the window?
- Are your own execs or spokespeople available? No exec earnings calls, no vacations in week of launch.

## Regional variations

- **UK/EU.** Avoid August entirely (summer vacation). Avoid week between Christmas and New Year.
- **China.** Avoid Chinese New Year (late January / mid-February) and Golden Week (first week of October).
- **India.** Avoid Diwali window (October-November).

## Crisis avoidance

Always have a soft-launch contingency in case major external news breaks the day of launch. Sheehan's rule: if the top story on CNN or the NYT front page is a national tragedy or major political event, post a short internal note that you're soft-launching (blog + in-product only, no press push, no paid amplification) and reassess in 48 hours.
