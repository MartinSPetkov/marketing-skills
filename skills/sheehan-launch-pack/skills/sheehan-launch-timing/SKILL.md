# Sheehan Launch Timing and Day-Of Coordination

A launch date is a promise. Pick it badly and every team downstream pays. Pick it well and the whole plan has a forcing function. Once set, protect it, because shifts compound.

## When to use this skill

Trigger on:

- "When should we launch?"
- "Is Friday a good launch day?"
- "Our date keeps slipping; help."
- "Who should be in the room on launch day?"
- "Give me a launch-day checklist."
- "How do I hit our launch date?"
- "Launch is 6 weeks out, what should the final month look like?"

Use this skill at two key points:

1. **When picking the date.** Before the GTM plan is locked.
2. **The final 4 weeks before launch.** Operational readiness and day-of coordination.

## Step 1: Pick a good launch date

### What makes a date "good"

- **Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday.** Monday is full of inbox catchup; Friday press coverage drops off by Saturday.
- **Avoids major industry events and news moments.** Don't launch during Dreamforce, AWS re:Invent, CES, unless you're exhibiting. Don't launch on the Tuesday after the Super Bowl; press is distracted.
- **Avoids earnings weeks for you and major customers.** Your exec can't focus during earnings.
- **Avoids US holidays and the week around them.** Thanksgiving week, Christmas week, July 4th week. Press is thin, attendance is thin.
- **Aligns with a customer event where possible.** Product launches tied to your own conference or a major trade show amplify naturally.
- **Gives you enough runway.** Tier 1: 10-12 weeks minimum. Tier 2: 4-6 weeks. Tier 3: 1-2 weeks.

### Best months for B2B launches

- **February-March.** Back from holidays, budgets fresh, full press attention.
- **April-May.** Strong, mid-quarter, no major holidays.
- **September-October.** Strong, post-Labor-Day, pre-holiday rush.

### Weak months

- **January.** Half the industry is recovering from holidays.
- **July-August.** Vacation season, press is thin.
- **December.** Only launch if you're ready for thin coverage.

### Best months for consumer launches

- **January** for fitness, self-improvement, productivity (New Year).
- **September** for back-to-school, work tools.
- **November** for gift-adjacent or holiday categories (but avoid Black Friday itself unless it's your story).

## Step 2: Set the date backwards from launch

Work backwards from the launch date. Every deliverable has a due date that back-solves into the plan.

Typical Tier 1 backward schedule:

| Milestone | Time before launch |
|---|---|
| Exec sign-off on tier and plan | 12 weeks |
| Target market and positioning locked | 10 weeks |
| Messaging house final | 8 weeks |
| Press list built, analyst briefings scheduled | 6-8 weeks |
| Sales training delivered | 4 weeks |
| Analyst briefings (2-3 weeks before press) | 4-5 weeks |
| Press embargo set | 2 weeks |
| Sales certified (Tier 1) | 2 weeks |
| All external assets final | 1 week |
| Dress rehearsal walkthrough | 3-5 days |
| Launch day | 0 |

See `references/backward-schedule-tier1.md` for the full week-by-week schedule.

## Step 3: Protect the date

Dates slip because of predictable pressures:

- Engineering hits a bug or scope creep.
- Legal asks for changes to press or blog copy with 48 hours to go.
- An exec wants to "get one more round of feedback" on messaging at T-2 weeks.
- A major customer event shifts and the team wants to align.

Protect the date with these rules:

- **Set a no-change date.** After T-3 weeks on Tier 1, no messaging or positioning changes. After T-1 week, no copy changes. Exec sign-off required to break this.
- **Lock the RACI.** If ownership is fuzzy, decisions drag.
- **Drive decisions to closure in the weekly sync.** "Let's discuss offline" on a decision under 2 weeks out is a warning sign.
- **Go/no-go meeting at T-1 week.** 30 minutes with the core team and exec sponsor. Single decision: ship or slip?
- **Buffer 2-3 days in the schedule.** Not at the end, in the middle. Things will slip; plan for it without moving the date.

## Step 4: Name the day-of core team

The launch day core team is smaller than the full launch team. 4-7 people who'll be on-call for issues during launch day.

Roles:

- Launch lead (PMM). Overall coordination, exec communication.
- PM. Product issues.
- Eng lead. Product reliability.
- Growth / Lifecycle. External email and paid.
- Content / Comms. Blog, social, press response.
- Support lead. Customer-facing issue triage.
- CS lead (for Tier 1). Top-account hand-holding.

The rest of the launch team is informed, not on-call.

Set up:

- Dedicated Slack channel or war room.
- Shared document for known issues.
- Rotating watch list for the first 8-24 hours.

## Step 5: The launch-day checklist

Sheehan's launch-day checklist covers the hours leading up to launch, launch moment, and first 24 hours.

### Launch day minus 1

- Freeze external assets.
- Confirm all campaigns are scheduled correctly.
- Confirm press release distribution time.
- Confirm in-product announcement trigger.
- Internal all-hands 5-min preview.
- Test all launch day links (homepage, blog, product page, sign-up).

### Launch day morning (before publish)

- PMM in the war room by 7am local time.
- Confirm press embargo release time.
- Final check on homepage, blog, in-product banners.
- Sales team has the day-1 FAQ in hand.

### Launch moment (T-0)

- Homepage updates go live.
- Blog post publishes.
- Press release distributes.
- External email queues fire.
- In-product announcement enables.
- Slack: launch moment post to the company.

### First hour

- Verify all channels are live.
- Watch for 404s, broken flows, payment issues.
- First press pickup monitoring.
- Social posts go live on schedule.

### First 8 hours

- Sales team feedback monitoring (questions, objections).
- Support ticket trend monitoring.
- Early metrics snapshot: signups, page views, press.
- Internal celebration moment (email, swag, cake).

### First 24 hours

- Day-1 metrics summary posted.
- Any issues escalated and triaged.
- Adjustments made to paid campaigns based on early CTR/CPA.
- Follow-up press outreach for pickups that missed the embargo window.

## Step 6: The 10 tips to hit your launch date

Sheehan's field-tested rules:

1. **Lock the tier early.** If tier debates happen at T-4 weeks, you're already behind.
2. **Name the launch lead early.** Launches without a single accountable owner slip.
3. **Hold a dress rehearsal.** T-5 days, walk the entire launch day in real time. Reveals broken things cheaply.
4. **Set a messaging no-change date.** T-3 weeks, no rewrites. Exec sign-off required to change.
5. **Certify sales reps by T-2 weeks.** Reps who can't pitch can't help.
6. **Build slack into the schedule.** 2-3 buffer days mid-plan, not at the end.
7. **Run weekly syncs with a green/yellow/red readout.** Yellow or red on any section for two weeks means escalate.
8. **Agree the go/no-go criteria in writing at T-8 weeks.** So T-1 week isn't a surprise.
9. **Publish the launch date internally and externally early.** Once your press agency has told reporters, moving the date has a real cost. Use that.
10. **Write the day-of runbook two weeks before launch.** Not the morning of.

## Output format

When asked to help pick a launch date, produce:

```
# Launch date recommendation

## Recommended date
[Specific date] - [Day of week] - [Rationale]

## Alternates
[2-3 alternate dates with tradeoffs]

## Avoid dates
[Conflicts to avoid and why]

## Backward schedule
[Week by week plan from today to launch]
```

When asked to coordinate launch day, produce:

```
# Launch day plan

## Launch date and time
[Date, time, timezone]

## Core team on-call
[Named people, roles, contact info]

## Day-minus-1 checklist
[...]

## Launch moment checklist (T-0)
[...]

## First hour / 8 hours / 24 hours
[...]

## Go/no-go criteria
[Written criteria, who signs off]
```

## What to avoid

- Never launch on a Monday or Friday unless you have a specific reason.
- Never commit to a date without a backward schedule that back-solves.
- Never let the messaging change in the last 2 weeks without exec sign-off.
- Never run a launch day without a named core team on-call.
- Never skip the dress rehearsal on Tier 1.
- Never treat the launch date as a suggestion. If it slips, debrief why and prevent the cause next time.
