# External promotion channels

Pick from this menu based on tier and budget. Don't try to run every channel on every launch.

| Channel | When to use | Cost | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Organic SEO | Long-term strategic investment to surface the product in search. | $$$ | 1 |
| Press (PR agency or internal PR) | Major product or company launch. Skilled pitching beats "emailing reporters." | $$$$ | 1 |
| Written case studies and testimonials | If beta customers will speak for you. Quote with logo is low-lift; full written case study is higher. | $–$$ | 1 |
| Paid digital (SEM, display, video ads) | New market or new site with little organic pull. | $$$$ | 1 |
| Video case study | High-impact. Needs scripting, videographer, editor. | $–$$$ | 1 |
| In-product messaging for product releases | Give existing customers a heads-up inside the product. Tools: Pendo, Amplitude, AppCues. | $–$$ | 1, 2 |
| Blogs and social posts | Drive awareness and build SEO. Should be ongoing, not just launch-day. | $ | 1, 2 |
| Content update for website | Mandatory if the site exists. Update product pages as part of launch. | $–$$$ | 1, 2 |
| External email announcement | Always a staple. Build the list constantly. | $ | 1, 2 |
| Help center update | Update knowledge base when new features ship. Helps promotion and support. | Free | 1, 2, 3 |

## Sequencing

For Tier 1, plan press first (3-6 weeks of pre-briefings), then website and email same day, then social and paid in the hours after. For Tier 2, website and email are the anchor; social and blog support them. For Tier 3, help center and changelog are enough.

## Press caveat

Press is never guaranteed. Build the launch to work without it. If press lands, it's a bonus.
