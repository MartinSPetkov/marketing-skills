# Example: launch team for a 3-person startup

Launch: a seed-stage B2B SaaS adding a new pricing tier. 4 weeks out. Three full-time people.

## The team

- Alex (CEO + founder)
- Jordan (CTO + PM + eng lead)
- Sam (Head of Everything Else: marketing, sales, CS, support)

## Launch lead

Sam. Doubles as PMM. No dedicated PMM hire yet.

## Compressed RACI

One page, not a grid. Too much structure is overhead for a team this size.

- Product readiness: Jordan (R+A). Ships by Day 1. Alex sign-off.
- Positioning and messaging: Sam (R+A). Alex reviews, CEO signs off.
- Pricing: Sam + Alex. Joint decision. Sam writes it up.
- Launch blog + homepage: Sam (R+A).
- External email: Sam (R+A). List: 1,200 signups.
- Sales comm: N/A. No sales team. Sam fields leads directly.
- CS comm: Sam. Writes a 200-word customer email.
- Internal comms: informal. Weekly 15-min sync between all three.
- Debrief: Sam writes 1 page, 2 weeks after launch.

## Meeting cadence

- Launch kickoff: 30 min. All three at a whiteboard. Discuss goals, split work, write dates.
- Weekly sync: 15 min on Mondays.
- Launch day: Slack celebration post, maybe a team dinner.

## What they skip

- RACI grid. Three people, three roles.
- Exec update. The exec team is two of the three people.
- Sales certification. There are no sales reps.
- PR agency. Sam writes a short post and pitches it to 5 outlets by email.
- Analyst briefings. Not at this stage.
- Rolling thunder plan. Maybe a case study in 60 days.

## What they can't skip

- Written GTM plan. Even if it's one page, write it. Three people still need to agree on the target.
- Sales comm, even without sales. Sam needs to answer customer emails consistently.
- CS outreach. Existing customers (all 40 of them) get a personal email from Sam.
- Debrief. The hardest part of being small is repeating your own mistakes. Write down what you'd do differently.

## Key principle

The Sheehan framework scales down, not out. For a 3-person team, the list of questions ("Who is this for?" "How will we know it worked?" "Who owns what?") is the same. The deliverables just get smaller.

A 200-word blog post is still a launch. A 40-person customer email is still CS outreach. A 15-min team sync is still a weekly launch meeting.

Don't skip the questions. Shrink the answers.
