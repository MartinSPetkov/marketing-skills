# PMM versus PM: who owns what

The most predictable friction on a launch is PMM and PM disagreeing over who owns the story, the customer, or the positioning. Settle it once in writing and point back to it when it flares up.

## The simple frame

- **PM owns the product.** What gets built, when it ships, who tests it, how it works.
- **PMM owns the market.** Who it's for, how we talk about it, how we sell it, how we measure its market impact.

## Detailed split

### What the PM owns

- Roadmap and prioritization.
- Product specs and engineering coordination.
- Release dates and scope decisions.
- Internal product documentation (eng docs, architecture notes).
- Beta program logistics (who's in, what they test, how feedback gets collected).
- Feature-level competitive analysis (does competitor X have this feature?).
- Product discovery research (do people want this feature?).
- Release notes (what changed, technical detail).

### What the PMM owns

- Launch tier decision.
- GTM plan end-to-end.
- Target market definition and personas for the launch.
- Positioning and messaging (with exec sign-off).
- Competitive positioning (how we beat Mixpanel in a deal, not whether our feature is better).
- Pricing (with Finance).
- Sales and CS enablement.
- External content direction (blog, press, social).
- Analyst relations.
- Launch metrics and post-launch analysis.
- Internal launch communications.

### What's shared

- **Customer research.** PM runs discovery research ("what should we build?"). PMM runs target-market research ("who is this for and how do they buy?"). Same customers, different questions. Share the calendar so customers don't get called twice in a week.
- **Beta customer selection.** PM picks based on product fit and technical readiness. PMM picks based on story potential (will they give a quote, be a case study?). Overlap the lists.
- **Messaging.** PM contributes feature facts, use cases, and product POV. PMM turns it into positioning, headlines, and benefit language. PMM is Accountable; PM is Consulted.

## How to resolve disputes

When PMM and PM clash on a specific deliverable, use the RACI:

1. Is the deliverable about the product itself? PM is Accountable.
2. Is the deliverable about how the product shows up in the market? PMM is Accountable.
3. Still unclear? Escalate to the launch sponsor (usually CMO or VP Product). Pick one. Move.

Do not split Accountability down the middle. "Joint owners" means no owner.

## The "voice of the customer" trap

Both PMM and PM think they own the customer. Both are right. The difference is which customer and for what.

- PM talks to prospects and users to decide what to build.
- PMM talks to prospects, users, churned customers, and non-buyers to decide how to sell what was built.

Different questions, different outputs. Share notes, don't fight for the title.

## The "story" trap

Both want to own "the story of the launch." The resolution:

- PM owns the product story: what it does, how it works, why we built it this way.
- PMM owns the customer story: who it helps, why it matters to them, how it fits into their work.

The PMM story is the one that goes on the homepage. The PM story is the one that goes in the blog post's "how we built it" section.

## When to merge the roles

Small teams often have one person doing both. That's fine. The division above still holds inside one person's head; it just doesn't need a meeting. The risk is under-investing in market-facing work because product-facing work feels more urgent. Keep at least a day a week on pure PMM tasks.

## When to split the roles

Hire a dedicated PMM when:

- You have at least 2 PMs and no PMM.
- Launches are regularly missing the mark on positioning, pricing, or sales readiness.
- Product ships but revenue doesn't follow.
- The CEO is doing the positioning work by default.

A PMM pays for themselves in the first year if any of those are true.
