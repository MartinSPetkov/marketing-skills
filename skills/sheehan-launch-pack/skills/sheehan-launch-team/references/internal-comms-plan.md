# Internal communications plan

The 10% rule: about a tenth of launch effort goes into telling people inside the company what's happening. Under-invest here and the launch lands quietly inside the walls, even if it's loud outside.

## Why internal comms matter

- Sales reps who don't know about the launch don't pitch it.
- CS reps who are caught off guard give customers a bad first impression.
- Execs who feel out of the loop show up in launch week asking questions that should have been answered in week 2.
- Marketing and design who don't see the plan duplicate work or miss their own deadlines.

## Cadence for a Tier 1 launch

| Time from launch | Touchpoint | Owner | Audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-12 weeks | Launch kickoff at all-hands | PMM | Whole company |
| T-12 weeks | #launch-[name] Slack channel opens | PMM | Launch team + interested parties |
| T-12 weeks | First written update posted | PMM | Company channel |
| T-10 weeks | Monthly exec update in exec staff | PMM | Exec team |
| T-8 weeks | Roadshow begins (sales regional meetings) | PMM | Regional sales teams |
| T-8 weeks | Written update #2 | PMM | Company channel |
| T-6 weeks | Written update #3 | PMM | Company channel |
| T-6 weeks | Monthly exec update | PMM | Exec team |
| T-4 weeks | Sales training session | PMM + Sales lead | Sales team |
| T-4 weeks | Written update #4 | PMM | Company channel |
| T-2 weeks | Certification deadline (Tier 1) | Sales managers | Sales reps |
| T-2 weeks | Written update #5 | PMM | Company channel |
| T-1 week | 5-minute launch preview at all-hands | PMM | Whole company |
| T-1 week | Final exec update | PMM | Exec team |
| Launch day | Internal email + swag drop | PMM | Whole company |
| Launch day | Slack announcement in general | PMM | Whole company |
| T+2 weeks | First metrics read-out at all-hands | PMM | Whole company |
| T+4 weeks | Day-30 metrics post | PMM | Company channel |
| T+6 weeks | Post-launch debrief | PMM | Launch team |
| T+8 weeks | Debrief findings shared | PMM | Exec team + launch team |

For Tier 2, compress to half the touchpoints. For Tier 3, keep just kickoff, launch day announcement, and 30-day update.

## The written update template

Post in the company Slack channel every 2 weeks. Under 200 words.

```
Launch update: [Name] | T-[X] weeks

Where we are
[One sentence on status: green / yellow / red]

What happened this week
- [Deliverable shipped]
- [Deliverable shipped]

What's up next
- [Next milestone, owner, date]
- [Next milestone, owner, date]

Help we need
- [Specific ask, or "none"]

Where to learn more
[Link to GTM plan, messaging, or wiki page]
```

## The exec update template

15 minutes in exec staff. Walking slide deck, not a read-aloud doc.

Slides:

1. Launch at a glance: name, date, tier, one-line description.
2. Goals: the numbers we committed to.
3. Status: green / yellow / red on each of the four checklist sections.
4. Risks: top 3, with mitigation.
5. One ask (headcount, budget, exec intro, decision needed).

## The all-hands moment

Launches need three all-hands moments:

1. **Kickoff.** At T-12 weeks (Tier 1) or T-6 (Tier 2). 5 minutes. Walk through what's launching, why, and the goal. Name the launch lead.
2. **Preview.** At T-1 week. 5 minutes. Remind the company what's coming, show one piece of creative, name the day.
3. **Results.** At T+2 weeks. 10 minutes. Show early metrics, highlight a customer quote, thank the team by name.

## The launch-day moment

The launch day itself is a party, not a meeting. Budget allowing:

- Branded swag delivered to everyone's desk or mailed to home addresses.
- Custom Slack emoji for the launch.
- Short video thanking the team, shared in Slack.
- Team lunch or virtual happy hour.

Sheehan's point: launches are hard, teams burn out. The celebration matters. Skipping it to "be serious" sends the wrong signal for the next launch.

## When internal comms fail

Three tells:

1. Sales reps ask questions in launch week that were answered in the FAQ 6 weeks ago.
2. An exec emails asking "what's the status on this launch?" during launch week.
3. A major customer-facing team (CS, support, sales ops) finds out from a customer.

If any of these happen, don't just answer the question. Update the plan. The gap repeats next launch.
