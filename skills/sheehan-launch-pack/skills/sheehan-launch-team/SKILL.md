# Sheehan Launch Team Alignment

Launches fail when roles are fuzzy or internal comms are an afterthought. The PMM owns the launch. Everyone else has clear, named jobs. And the team spends roughly 10% of launch effort on internal communication, because a launch nobody knows about internally underperforms externally.

## When to use this skill

Trigger on:

- "Who owns what on this launch?"
- "Set up a RACI for the launch."
- "PMM and PM are stepping on each other."
- "Sales says nobody told them about the launch."
- "We need an internal launch comms plan."
- "How do I run a launch kickoff?"
- "Our launch team can't get aligned."

Run this AFTER the GTM plan exists. Role assignments should map to the four checklist sections in the GTM plan.

## Step 1: Name the launch lead

One person. Named. Accountable. Almost always the PMM.

The launch lead:

- Owns the GTM plan end to end.
- Chairs the weekly launch team sync.
- Writes and sends every internal update.
- Is the single point of escalation.

If there's no PMM, name whoever is playing the PMM role. Don't leave it "marketing" or "the team." Ambiguity kills launches.

## Step 2: Build the RACI

Use the RACI model: Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed.

- **Responsible (R):** Does the work.
- **Accountable (A):** Signs off. One per task.
- **Consulted (C):** Gives input before it's done.
- **Informed (I):** Told after it's done.

Every major deliverable on the GTM plan gets a RACI row. See `references/raci-template.md` for a filled-out example.

Core roles on most launches:

- PMM: Accountable for the launch overall, Responsible for messaging, positioning, sales enablement, internal comms, external content direction.
- PM: Responsible for product readiness, beta, release notes. Consulted on messaging.
- Eng lead: Responsible for ship date and quality. Informed on GTM.
- Design: Responsible for launch creative, product UI.
- Content/Copy: Responsible for blog, website, press release drafts.
- Growth/Demand Gen: Responsible for paid, email, lifecycle.
- Sales lead: Responsible for sales readiness, certification. Consulted on messaging.
- CS lead: Responsible for customer comms to existing accounts.
- PR/Comms: Responsible for press strategy and pitch.
- Executive sponsor: Accountable for go/no-go at the exec level.

## Step 3: Set the cadence

Three meetings, no more:

1. **Launch kickoff (once, 8-12 weeks before launch for Tier 1, 4-6 for Tier 2).** Walk the whole team through the GTM plan, RACI, goals, and timeline. Invite everyone involved, including sales and CS. Make it memorable. Sheehan suggests swag, a theme, or a short video.

2. **Weekly launch team sync (30 minutes, from kickoff to launch).** PMM-led. Agenda: status of each checklist section, risks, decisions needed. Everyone reads the checklist before the meeting; the meeting is for blockers, not status reading.

3. **Monthly executive update (15 minutes in exec staff).** PMM shares progress, risks, and one ask. Don't skip this; execs who don't see updates invent concerns on launch day.

Async channels:

- Slack channel: `#launch-[name]` for the whole team, `#launch-[name]-core` for the leads.
- Shared folder with the GTM plan, messaging, assets, and the checklist.
- Weekly written update from the PMM to the broader company.

## Step 4: The 10% internal communications rule

Spend about 10% of your launch effort on internal comms. If the team does 40 hours of work on a launch, 4 go to telling people inside the company what's happening.

What "internal comms" covers:

- Launch kickoff at all-hands.
- Monthly exec update.
- Biweekly company-wide written update.
- Sales roadshow (PMM presents the launch in every regional sales meeting).
- Launch day internal email + swag or celebration.
- Day-30 metrics post.

Rule: sales, CS, and support should hear about the launch three times before it ships. Marketing, product, and eng should hear about it weekly.

See `references/internal-comms-plan.md` for the full cadence and templates.

## Step 5: Resolve the PMM-PM overlap

Predictable tension: PMM and PM both feel they own "the voice of the customer" and "the story."

Sheehan's division:

- **PM owns:** the product itself, what ships, release dates, scope, technical decisions, beta program management, internal product documentation.
- **PMM owns:** how the product shows up in the market, positioning, messaging, pricing (with Finance), target market definition, launch tier and plan, sales and CS enablement, external content, metrics for the launch.
- **Shared:** customer research (PM runs discovery, PMM runs target-market research for launch), competitive intel (PM tracks feature-level, PMM tracks positioning-level), beta customer selection.

If the tension is still live, use the RACI to force the conversation. Every deliverable has one Accountable owner. Pick, move on.

## Step 6: Sales and CS enablement

Sales and CS are the launch's amplifiers. If they aren't ready, the launch doesn't land.

Sales enablement flow:

1. Sales lead named as RACI owner for sales readiness.
2. PMM drafts sales comm doc (internal FAQ), pitch script, one-sheet, slide additions. See `references/sales-enablement-checklist.md`.
3. Training session 2-4 weeks before launch.
4. For Tier 1: certification. Every rep records themselves pitching, PMM or sales manager scores against a rubric. No pitch, no pipeline.
5. Day-1 Slack channel for live questions during the launch week.

CS enablement flow:

1. CS lead named.
2. PMM drafts existing-customer talking points and outreach templates.
3. CS maps which accounts need proactive outreach (top accounts, at-risk accounts, likely upsell accounts).
4. CS runs outreach in the 2 weeks around launch.

## Step 7: Post-launch debrief

Two weeks after launch, 90 minutes with the core launch team. Three questions:

- What worked?
- What didn't?
- What would we do differently next time?

Write it up. Share with the team. Use it for the next launch. Without a debrief, every launch makes the same mistakes the last one made.

## Output format

When asked to set up launch team alignment, produce:

```
# Launch team alignment: [Launch name]

## Launch lead
[Name, role]

## RACI
[Table of deliverables with R, A, C, I columns]

## Meeting cadence
- Kickoff: [date, attendees, format]
- Weekly sync: [day, time, attendees]
- Monthly exec update: [which meeting, owner]

## Internal comms plan
[Timeline of internal touchpoints from T-8 weeks to T+30 days]

## Sales and CS enablement
- Sales training: [date, format, certification yes/no]
- CS outreach: [date range, target accounts, owner]

## Debrief
[Date, owner]
```

## What to avoid

- Never leave the launch lead ambiguous. One name.
- Never skip the kickoff. The hour you save becomes a month of confusion.
- Never skimp on internal comms. A launch nobody knows about internally underperforms.
- Never let PMM and PM fight over ownership in meetings. Fix it in the RACI once and move on.
- Never run a launch without sales certification for Tier 1. Reps who can't pitch don't pitch.
- Never skip the debrief. You'll repeat every mistake.
