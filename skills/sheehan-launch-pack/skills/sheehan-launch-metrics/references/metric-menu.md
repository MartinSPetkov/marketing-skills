# Metric menu

Full menu of launch metrics by category. Pick 5-7 total across categories. Tune the target and window based on tier and product type.

## Adoption

Who's using the new thing?

- **Activated users.** Users who hit the "aha moment" (first value event). Define the event up front.
- **Activation rate.** Activated / total target population.
- **Feature adoption rate.** % of target users using the launched feature at least once.
- **Daily / weekly / monthly active users of the feature.**
- **Time to first use.** Days from signup or launch to first use of the new feature.

## Revenue

Did money move?

- **Paid signups / new accounts.**
- **New ARR / MRR.**
- **Upgrade rate.** % of existing base moving to the new tier.
- **Average contract value (ACV).**
- **Expansion revenue per account.**
- **Churn rate.** Especially if the launch was retention-focused.

## Awareness

Did anyone hear about it?

- **Press mentions in target publications.**
- **Tier 1 press mentions.** Count only pubs you listed as targets.
- **Share of voice versus named competitors.**
- **Direct traffic to launch pages.**
- **Branded search lift.** (New Google search volume on product/brand terms.)
- **Social mentions / reach.**
- **Analyst inclusion.** Mentioned in analyst note or quadrant within X months.

## Engagement

Are they coming back?

- **7-day retention.** Of users who activated in week 1, how many used it again in week 2?
- **30-day retention.**
- **NPS or CSAT change post-launch.**
- **Feature depth.** Average number of distinct actions per user in the new feature.

## Sales

Did sales pipeline move?

- **Pipeline generated from the launch.** Opportunities tagged to the launch campaign.
- **Deal cycle time.** Has it shortened since the launch?
- **Win rate on new deals.** Has it moved?
- **Sales certification rate.** % of reps certified on the new pitch.
- **Pitch-to-demo conversion.** If the launch changed the deck, does it convert better?

## Customer Success

Did existing customers benefit?

- **Gross retention rate.**
- **Net retention rate.**
- **Support ticket volume per customer.** Up = confusion. Down or stable = good.
- **Customer satisfaction on the launch specifically.** Post-launch survey.
- **Expansion or upsell rate.** Existing customers adding new plan or seats.

## By launch type

### New product launch (Tier 1)

Adoption + revenue + awareness heavy. Usually 6-7 metrics split across these three.

Example set:

- 500 paid signups in 90 days (revenue).
- $2.5M new ARR in 6 months (revenue).
- 12 press mentions in Tier 1 pubs in launch month (awareness).
- 40% of existing base upgrades in 6 months (revenue / adoption).
- 30-day retention on new feature >= 40% (engagement).
- Sales certification 90%+ by T-2 weeks (sales).

### Feature launch (Tier 2)

Adoption and engagement heavy. Revenue is a secondary input.

Example set:

- 60% of target users try the feature in 30 days (adoption).
- 35% use it weekly at day 60 (engagement).
- Retention on target users up by 2 points in 90 days (retention).
- Support tickets on the feature < 5 per 1,000 users (CS).
- Internal: 80% of sales can describe the feature after training.

### Update or improvement (Tier 3)

Minimal. 2-3 metrics.

Example set:

- Support ticket volume on the updated feature drops by X% (CS).
- NPS on the affected area up by Y points (CS).

## By product type

### B2B SaaS

Lean on pipeline, ARR, adoption-by-account, sales certification.

### Consumer app

Lean on installs, activation rate, 7- and 30-day retention, revenue per user.

### Developer tool

Lean on sign-ups, time to first API call, weekly active developers, community engagement.

### Internal-only launch (to existing customers)

Lean on upgrade rate, retention, support volume, existing-customer NPS.

## Rules for picking metrics

- Tie each metric to one of the stakeholder success statements.
- Prefer metrics with a known baseline. "New ARR" is useless if you don't know what baseline ARR was.
- Prefer counting metrics over rate metrics when numbers are small. "5 new enterprise logos" beats "enterprise conversion rate."
- Avoid metrics you can't measure reliably. If tracking isn't wired, don't commit to the number.
- Prefer metrics you can see weekly, not only at day 90.

## Warning signs

- Too many metrics. If the scorecard has 15 rows, cut 8.
- Metric without a target. "Track press mentions" is not a goal; "12 press mentions in Tier 1" is.
- Metric without a source. If you can't name the report, you can't report on it.
- Vanity metrics. Impressions, reach, "eyeballs" are noise unless tied to action.
