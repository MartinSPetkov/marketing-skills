# Stakeholder metrics map

Every launch has multiple audiences inside the company. Each cares about different numbers. Map the stakeholders, then map the metrics.

## The stakeholders

### CEO

**Cares about:** Top-line impact. Revenue, market position, strategic story.

**Typical success statement:** "This launch delivers on the FY plan by contributing $X to new ARR and establishing us as the category leader."

**Metrics they care about:**

- New ARR attributable to the launch.
- Pipeline generated.
- Press and analyst coverage in tier-1 pubs.
- Existing customer retention and expansion.

**What not to bring them:** Engagement metrics, support tickets, granular funnel data. They'll nod but move on.

### CMO

**Cares about:** Category story, awareness, leads, campaign efficiency.

**Typical success statement:** "This launch lands our category narrative and brings in qualified demand at target CAC."

**Metrics they care about:**

- Share of voice.
- Press mentions in target pubs.
- Launch page traffic and conversion.
- Cost per lead, cost per opportunity.
- Direct / branded search lift.
- MQL to SQL conversion from launch campaigns.

**What not to bring them:** Raw impressions. "Reach" without downstream conversion.

### CRO / VP Sales

**Cares about:** Pipeline, win rate, deal cycle, rep readiness.

**Typical success statement:** "Sales can pitch the new product consistently and closes the gap against [competitor] in deals."

**Metrics they care about:**

- Pipeline from launch.
- Sales certification rate.
- Deal cycle time.
- Win rate versus named competitors.
- Pitch-to-demo conversion.

**What not to bring them:** Press mentions. They'll assume marketing is doing its job and move on.

### CPO / VP Product

**Cares about:** Adoption, engagement, quality.

**Typical success statement:** "The new feature is being used at the target rate and isn't creating a support or stability problem."

**Metrics they care about:**

- Activation rate.
- Feature adoption and retention.
- Support ticket volume on the new feature.
- Bug reports and sev-1 incidents.
- Customer feedback on the feature.

**What not to bring them:** Press mentions. Revenue attribution (usually).

### VP CS

**Cares about:** Customer impact, retention, expansion, support load.

**Typical success statement:** "Our existing customers see value in the new product, and it doesn't spike our support volume."

**Metrics they care about:**

- Existing customer upgrade / upsell rate.
- Net retention rate.
- Support ticket volume.
- NPS on the new product specifically.
- Churn rate (especially for Tier 1 retention-focused launches).

**What not to bring them:** Press mentions, new-logo metrics.

### CFO / Finance

**Cares about:** Unit economics and budget adherence.

**Typical success statement:** "The launch hits its revenue number and stays within budget."

**Metrics they care about:**

- Revenue versus plan.
- Launch budget actual versus plan.
- Cost per acquired account.
- Payback period.

**What not to bring them:** Engagement metrics, feature-level data.

### Sales Engineering / CS Engineering

**Cares about:** Product reliability and demo-ability.

**Typical success statement:** "The new product is demoable without hacks and works in customer environments."

**Metrics they care about:**

- Demo failure rate.
- Onboarding / activation friction.
- Documentation completeness.
- Open sev-1s.

### Support

**Cares about:** Ticket volume and resolution time.

**Typical success statement:** "We're not drowning in new tickets and we can resolve them in SLA."

**Metrics they care about:**

- Tickets per customer / per day.
- First-response time.
- Resolution time.
- Open bug backlog.

## Using the map

Before launch:

1. List every stakeholder who'll judge this launch.
2. For each, write their one-sentence success statement.
3. For each, pick the 1-2 metrics they'll actually watch.
4. Consolidate across stakeholders. You'll often find 5-7 unique metrics covering everyone.
5. Get each stakeholder to sign off on their sentence and their metrics.

After launch:

1. Tailor the day 7, 30, 60, 90 report to highlight the metric each stakeholder cares about.
2. Don't assume a generic report works for all. The CEO reads the first paragraph; the CPO reads the adoption table; the CRO jumps to pipeline.
3. Use a one-page summary with links to deeper cuts for each audience.

## When stakeholders disagree

Common: CEO wants revenue, CPO wants adoption, CRO wants pipeline. All valid, all different windows.

Resolution:

- Use the launch tier to set priority. Tier 1 launches usually lead with revenue and awareness; Tier 2 leads with adoption; Tier 3 is support and CS quality.
- When stakeholders pull in conflicting directions, escalate to the launch sponsor. One call resolves months of report misalignment.
