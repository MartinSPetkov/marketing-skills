# Day 30 launch report template

One to two pages. Honest. Delivered 30 days after launch. Shared with exec team, launch team, and the broader company.

## Template

```
# Day 30 launch report: [Launch name]

**Launch date:** [Date]
**Tier:** [1 / 2 / 3]
**Launch lead:** [Name]

## Headline

[One paragraph. Did we hit the top goal? What's the overall read: on track, ahead, behind, or missed? No hedging.]

## Results

| Metric | Target (90d) | Day 30 actual | On track? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Metric 1] | [Number] | [Number] | Y/N | [One line] |
| [Metric 2] | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| ...

## What worked

- [Specific thing that worked, with evidence. "Sales certification hit 95% by T-2 weeks; demos in the first 30 days averaged 3 per rep per week."]
- [Specific thing that worked.]
- [Specific thing that worked.]

## What didn't

- [Specific thing that didn't, with honest cause. "Press coverage came in at 6 mentions, below the target of 12. Top cause: two of the tier-1 pubs we targeted are running industry retrospectives and declined new product stories this month."]
- [Specific thing that didn't.]

## What we're changing

- [Specific action in the next 30 days. "Shifting $40K of paid budget from LinkedIn (lower CPA than expected) to retargeting."]
- [Specific action.]

## Customer stories

- [One or two quotes or short stories from real customers using the new product.]

## Thanks

[Name the people who made it happen. Publicly.]

## Next checkpoint

[Date of day 60 update. Link to live dashboard.]
```

## Rules for writing it

### The headline paragraph

- Lead with the score. Don't bury bad news in paragraph four.
- One of four: "on track," "ahead," "behind but recoverable," "missed."
- No vague language. "Off to a promising start" means nothing.

### The results table

- Pull target and actual from the original GTM plan goals. Don't revise targets mid-flight without noting it.
- Day 30 actual may be a fraction of the 90-day target. That's fine. What matters is whether the trajectory is on pace.
- "On track?" column: Y or N. No Maybes.

### What worked, what didn't

- 3-5 items per section. More is noise.
- Every item has evidence, not opinion.
- Be specific about causes. Generic "marketing did well" or "sales was slow" is useless.

### What we're changing

- Concrete actions with owners and dates where possible.
- If we're changing nothing, say so, and defend why.

### Customer stories

- Real, sourced, with permission where identifying.
- Anonymous quotes are OK; invented ones are never OK.

### Thanks

- Name the people. "Thanks to the launch team" is a pat on the back. "Thanks to Raj for the beta support work and Dev for the blog post that Hacker News picked up" is recognition.

## Distribution

- Post in the company Slack channel.
- Email the exec team with a 3-sentence summary + link.
- Present the headline and top 3 items at the next all-hands.

## Common failures

- **No report at all.** The most common. Fix: put it on the launch lead's calendar at launch day.
- **Spin.** Hiding the miss with qualifiers. Reads as defensive. Better to own it.
- **Data dump.** 15 metrics, no interpretation. Cut.
- **No learnings.** "Everything went great, we'll keep doing what we're doing." Either a lie or a sign no one's paying attention.

## Variants

- **Day 7 report:** 1 Slack post. Early read only. What shipped, what's the first signal, what are we watching.
- **Day 60 report:** shorter version of day 30. Focus on trend and adjustments.
- **Day 90 report:** final scorecard. Includes debrief findings. Shorter than day 30 because most of the story is known by then.
